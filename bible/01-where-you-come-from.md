# Where You Come From: A Character Bio Helper

*The Real Work. The lands around Drezen, for anyone writing a character who is standing in the
city in the autumn of 4713 AR and has not yet worked out why.*

This is a handout for building a backstory. It is not a chapter, not a rulebook, and not a
promise that any of it will still be true by Kuthona. It gathers what passes for fact about the
country around **Drezen** (the Worldwound, old Sarkoris, Mendev and its capital Nerosyan) from
Paizo's published setting and from the crusade's own chronicle at
[The Fifth Crusade](https://mattdahse.github.io/the-fifth-crusade/), and it stops at the present
day.

**Which source wins.** Where Paizo and the chronicle differ, the chronicle governs at this table.
Where the chronicle is silent, Paizo's setting stands. Where both are silent, make something up
and tell the DM, so that it can be written down and later contradicted by a dice roll.

**How to use it.** Skim §1 so you know what everyone in the city knows. Read the one or two
sections that match where your character is from. Then answer the questions in §8. The map of
Drezen is on this site's Map tab, in the same voice as this document.

**The general situation, for those in a hurry.** A god was due back in 4606 to settle everything.
He died instead. A hole opened where he was supposed to stand, and everything since has been
people building walls in front of the hole and demons taking the walls down, for a hundred and
seven years, with great sincerity on both sides. The demons are enjoying it more. You have been
hired to do the part of this that does not fit in a song.

## 1. The moment you are standing in

It is **Neth, 4713 AR**. Late autumn, with the first hard frosts on the Marchlands and the
southern horizon lit orange after dark. Everyone in Drezen carries these facts, mostly the way
one carries a bad tooth.

**Kenabres fell on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus.** Iomedae's own feast day. The Storm King came
over the walls, the silver dragon **Terendelev** died in the sky above the city, the great bridge
went down, and the wardstone in the Kite was shattered. The crusade's northern wall for a century
fell in an afternoon. Prelate **Hulrun** had spent fifty years burning people to keep the demons
out. The demons killed him first.

**The wardstones are gone.** A week later, at the **Gray Garrison**, four strangers out of the
rubble broke the corrupted fragment before the enemy could turn it against the army gathering at
the border, and every wardstone along the West Sellen went dark with it. The border that held for
seventy-five years is now a river. The power the stones held went into the four. The church has
ruled this a miracle. The Riftwardens have asked to see the paperwork.

**The four** are **Rabiah**, a sorcerer of sixteen; **Harlock Greyforge**, a half-orc paladin
who carries a sword that talks; **Lupenor Celest**, an elven archer; and **Varic Sarian**, a
priest of Sarenrae. Mendev calls them the Wardstone's Champions, and there is a ballad. You will
hear it in every tavern between here and Egede, sung by people who could not find Drezen on a
chart, and the verse about the forty at the door gets longer every month. The four are away in
the west now, days out of contact. They do not otherwise come into this document. You will not be
working for them. You will be working for the people they left behind, who are less famous and
pay in coin.

**Queen Galfrey rode north** to Kenabres herself, and sent the champions to Drezen with a hundred
volunteers. They marched out on the 30th of Arodus, took **Vilareth Ford** back on the road,
declined to walk into **Keeper's Canyon** in the dark, and came within sight of Drezen's walls on
the 6th of Rova. Most of the hundred are alive. Nobody had planned for that, and the barracks are
short of beds.

**Drezen changed hands on Fire Day, the 13th of Rova, 4713.** The citadel that fell to the demons
in 4638 is a crusader city again. The ***Sword of Valor***, the banner lost when it fell, was
carried out of the dungeons beneath it, flawless after seventy-five years in demon hands.
**Staunton Vhane**, the dwarf who betrayed the city three generations ago, is buried here. The
banner came through the occupation without a stain. Nothing else did.

**Sir Aldrich Thorne** came from the Queen the next day, by teleportation, with two guards. He
saw the city passed to **Irabeth Tirabade** to govern, declined a meal, and left the same
afternoon. He has not been back. Ask anyone in Drezen what the Queen's man thought of the place
and they will tell you how long he stayed.

**The crusade holds the walls and the citadel.** It does not yet hold every quarter. The people
holding the rest are hiring. You are what they hired.

If your character has been in Drezen more than a week, they have also heard: that the demons
themselves have posted a thousand platinum for a succubus somewhere in the west, more than the
crusade has ever offered for anything; that a shrine of Sarenrae two days down the western road
has been reopened and is kept by a priest named **Rothin Vald**; that the **South Bank** has not
been searched; and that **Horgus Gwerm**, the quartermaster, is browbeating the crown for grain.
His last dispatch is public knowledge because he read it aloud in the market first:

> *Your Majesty. Drezen holds. Drezen has four hundred mouths more than it had on the 13th and
> the same grain. I am told the crown's stores are committed to Kenabres. I am told a great many
> things. Send wheat, or send fewer people, or send Sir Aldrich back to explain it to them; he
> will not need long.*
>
> *Gwerm, Quartermaster.*

## 2. Mendev: the crusader state

**The land.** The northern end of the continent: snow-covered mountains, icy steppe, and the
great evergreen **Estrovian Forest** across the centre and south. North, the ice cliffs of the
Crown of the World. East, the **Lake of Mists and Veils**, which every crusader crossed to get
here. South, across the rivers, **Numeria**, a harsh country of savages and alien metal that
Mendevians want nothing to do with. West, the **West Sellen River**, and on its far bank the
Worldwound. There is almost no arable land. For a century the crusade has been fed by boats,
crusaders and grain coming up the Sellen and across the lake, arriving weekly at **Egede**. The
grain is inspected. The crusaders are not.

**Three kinds of Mendevian.** Nobody in Mendev is just "a Mendevian." Ask which.

- **The natives.** Descended from Iobarian exiles, semi-nomadic once, and cousins to the
  Sarkorians across the river. Many still keep the old **animist** faith or the **Green Faith**,
  quietly. The crusaders have treated them as second-class for a hundred years and in the Third
  Crusade burned them for heresy. A native Mendevian character has a grandmother who remembers
  who informed on whom. The crown kept no such list. The grandmothers did.
- **The crusaders and their children.** From Cheliax, Isger, Andoran, Taldor and everywhere
  else. The first wave were the finest people of the south, clerics and paladins and knights who
  sold their lands to come. By the Fourth Crusade the boats were bringing mercenaries,
  dispossessed nobles, political undesirables and men let out of prison on condition they went
  north, and these now outnumber the pious. A crusader's-child character can be third-generation
  Mendevian and still be a foreigner to the natives, whose great-uncle their grandfather may have
  denounced.
- **The Sarkorians.** Kellid refugees of the lost kingdom, three generations in exile. See §5.
  In Nerosyan they have a quarter of their own along the western wall, the **Fire Circle**.
  Others took the caves of **Saresp Mountain** in the far east, or scattered into Numeria,
  Ustalav and beyond.

And among them: **tieflings** in unusual numbers (Mendev is home to many asura-spawn, and the
Wound breeds pitborn); **dwarves** whose ancestors fled the lost Sky Citadel of **Jormurdun**
with a royal claim and a piece of a broken key, and who mostly live poor in the Fleshward slum of
Chesed, still holding the piece; halflings; half-elves; and beneath Kenabres, the **mongrelmen**,
the warped descendants of First Crusade soldiers whom the Wound changed and whom their own
comrades then tried to kill.

**Rule.** A monarchy under **Queen Galfrey**, the Sword of Iomedae, a Taldan woman who looks to
be in her middle forties and is over a hundred years old, held there by the sun orchid elixir.
Crowned in 4601 as a paladin of Aroden; five years later Aroden died, and she retrained herself
as Iomedae's paladin with the brisk practicality of a woman whose first god had let her down. She
ordered the retreat from Sarkoris in 4638 so the wardstones could be raised, and has been hated
for it since by the descendants of the people it kept alive. Her word is law within Nerosyan's
walls. Beyond them, every commander of every city, fort and army interprets her commands a little
differently. Until this summer the second most powerful person in the kingdom was **Prelate
Hulrun Shappok** of Kenabres, whose witch-burnings as a young man started the Third Crusade, and
who only in his last years began to understand the shame he had brought on the faith. He was
working on it.

**Faith.** **Iomedae**, the Inheritor, is the crusade. Her white-and-gold banner is the one all
crusaders nominally fight under, her cathedral is the centre of Nerosyan, and her holy day
**Armasse** is the day Kenabres fell. She inherited her portfolio from Aroden, who died on the
day he was due to return. The Chapel of the Argent Shield on the Egede road, once his, still
keeps a chair for him by the altar. Nobody sits in it. Nobody has moved it either. Beside her:
**Sarenrae**, whose priests march with the columns and heal what the columns leave; **Shelyn**,
**Desna**, **Erastil**, **Abadar**, **Pharasma**; **Gorum** in the barracks of Egede; **Cayden
Cailean**, whose neighbourhood in Nerosyan has the city's best-attended services. The Green Faith
and the animist ways of the natives survive, and are looked at sideways. Every Mendevian, pious
or not, knows the gesture: fingers to the lips, then pressed to the sword-pommel. Ask a Mendevian
what it does. Watch them do it again while they think.

Three sayings from any Mendevian kitchen:

> *Aroden is coming. Set a place.*
> *The wall is only as good as the priest is sober.*
> *Pray to Iomedae. Pay Abadar. Drink with Cayden. Bury with Pharasma. Ask the trees when nobody's looking.*

**Orders and outfits.** A crusader belongs to something, or pretends to.

- **The Eagle Watch**: the crusaders of Kenabres. **Irabeth Tirabade** commanded them at the
  Defender's Heart; **Anevia**, her wife, served among them.
- **The Order of Heralds**: curbed the Third Crusade's purges and turned the war back outward.
  Still the conscience of the crusade. See the table above for their reward.
- **The Riftwardens**: a secretive society of planar scholars who want the Worldwound *closed*,
  not merely held, based in Kenabres's **Librarium of the Broken Black Wing**, which has since
  partly collapsed. **Aravashnial**, an elven wizard blinded in the fall of Kenabres and since
  healed by the Queen's own priests, is one. Their mark is the Seeker's Spiral. Do not give it to
  a new character without asking.
- **Iomedae's Preservers**: the newest of the orders, founded this autumn by **Harlock
  Greyforge** and headquartered in Drezen. Five squads sworn to hold the city. Their recruiting
  bill is nailed to the citadel gate:
  > *IOMEDAE'S PRESERVERS seek the steadfast. Three days' trial. Bring your own boots. Food
  > provided on the third day. Those who leave before the third day are asked to return the
  > boots if they were not their own. Apply to Dame Elara Dawnstrider, who will know if you are
  > lying.*
- **The Kenabres Wolves**: the spies and scouts, **Aron Kir**'s people. He is Warden of Drezen
  now.
- **The Order of Holy Sentinels**: guardians of the ***Demonscope*** at **Clydwell Keep**, the
  greatest of the border forts. Every fort on the old wardstone line is named for a dead general.
- **The knightly orders**: the Emerald Sword, the Flaming Lance, the Rampant Dragon, the Sunrise
  Sword, the Guild of Diggers and Sappers, and dozens more. Some are ancient. Some were invented
  on the boat, because the heralds of Iomedae cannot check every newcomer's claim to a title.
  The heralds know this. The knights know the heralds know. A title is a lie everyone has agreed
  on, and Mendev has agreed on a great many.
- **The Low Templars**: the crusade coined the name for its mercenaries and sellswords, then
  noticed it had coined a name for most of the crusade, and kept it.
- **The Pathfinder Society**: in Nerosyan since the Queen gave them the Starrise Spire in 4707,
  sending expeditions into the Wound disguised as mercenary companies.
- **The inquisitors**: Hulrun's people. Fewer than they were, and quieter since Kenabres.

**Banners.** Any crusader stronghold is a riot of colour, flags from hundreds of clans, counties,
kingdoms and families competing for room. The banners that matter are earned on the field and
take the form of Iomedae's own symbols, the **sword**, the **shield** or the **sunburst**, granted
to soldiers of any rank. It is the one honour in Mendev that cannot be bought, and it is a piece
of cloth.

**Speech.** Mendevians speak **Taldane** (Common); the natives and the Sarkorians also speak
**Hallit**, the Kellid tongue, which has no written form. A **gaffle** or a **rip** is a scam.
There is no Mendevian word for an honest bargain. They use the Taldane, carefully.

**The wardstones, and their absence.** For seventy-five years the border was a line of
rune-carved menhirs along the West Sellen and the Moutray, fed with **nexovar** from the hills of
the Egelsee, maintained by daily prayer and a yearly ritual, and housed in a chain of forts.
Nothing chaotic or evil could pass between them, and no teleportation could cross the line. They
are gone since Arodus. The forts are still garrisoned. The prayers are still said every morning
over stones that no longer hear them. A character who served on the line has lost the whole
meaning of their posting and kept the posting. The pay still comes.

## 3. Nerosyan: the Diamond of the North

The capital, and the crusade's fortress. Before the Worldwound it was a run-down river town of
pirates; the First Crusade widened its streets, walled it, and gave it a holy purpose, and the
pirates' grandchildren are now the city guard. About sixty-five thousand people, nearly all human.

**Where it sits.** At the junction where the **Egelsee River** runs into the northernmost branch
of the **West Sellen**. The Egelsee is the city's only clean water; the Sellen comes down too
close to the Wound to drink. Everything outside the walls has been burned flat, by demonic raids
and by the defenders, so that nothing approaches unseen. Nobody now remembers which fires were
whose.

**How it is built.** A diamond between the two rivers, so that every wall has water in front of
it. The walls are twenty feet thick, and the wall-tops are *roofed*, sloped and cut with arrow
slits, because the enemy flies. At the centre stands the **Cruciform Cathedral** of Iomedae, and
the avenues run wide and straight from it so that a sortie can reach any wall in minutes, and so
that the cathedral can see every door. Four towers hold the points: the **Battle Tower** in the
north, the most heavily fortified structure in Mendev; the **Woundward Tower**, holding the
city's wardstone and its best archers; the **Confluence Tower**, guarding the rivers and
Crusader's Quay; and the **Egelsee Tower**, the cavalry's ground. Above it all burns the
**Starrise Spire**, older than the walls, with a ball of light floating over it that has not gone
out in centuries: a beacon to the crusade and a warning to the Wound, which has had a century to
get used to it.

**The districts.** Battle, Egelsee, Confluence and Woundward are the four quarters. Within them:
**Pilgrim's Rest**, where the endless stream of Iomedaean pilgrims lodges; **Bannerbright**,
where the orders keep their houses and their feuds; the **Two Cs**, Cayden Cailean's
neighbourhood, which is where the taverns are; the **Fire Circle** along the western wall, where
the Sarkorian refugees keep their shops and eating houses in the old style, facing the wall,
facing home. **Hank the Alerunner's** hideout is a tavern full of relics of the pirate days.
**Ancuin's Brewery** has been the crusaders' favourite for a hundred years and is the institution
with the best record in Mendev.

From the pamphlet handed to pilgrims at the Sellen Docks, fourth printing:

> *WELCOME TO NEROSYAN, DIAMOND OF THE NORTH. Pilgrims will find lodging in Pilgrim's Rest, at
> rates set by the crown. Pilgrims will find other lodging in the Two Cs, at rates set by the
> Two Cs. The Cathedral is open at all hours. The Woundward Tower is not a viewing platform.
> Please do not feed the Sarkorians; they have their own quarter and their own food and they
> would like you to know that. Recruiters are stationed at every gate and are not permitted to
> follow you into the Cathedral. They know this. Walk quickly.*

**Who runs it.** The Queen, from her palace, counselled by an angel named **Opaline**, who has
presumably seen the whole plan and is not sharing. Around her, **Lord Inquisitor Casori**,
**Captain Jaspar** of the Crusader Heralds, and Venture-Captain **Jorsal of Lauterbury** of the
Pathfinders.

**What has happened lately.** For years the city has been harried by things that come up the
rivers, merrow and scrags and grindylows in the service of the hezrou that rules ruined Storasta
downriver, and there are stories of a larger siege this year, turned back with the Pathfinders in
the thick of it, which the Pathfinders tell. The wagons of conscripts and supply have been
rolling north to Kenabres and on to Drezen since Rova. They leave Nerosyan with twelve conscripts
and reach Drezen with nine. The quartermaster's ledger records this as *shrinkage*.

**Bio hooks from Nerosyan.** A dock-hand who has unloaded a hundred boatloads of heroes and
watched none come back down. A cathedral acolyte who has never been outside the walls and
believes everything, so far. A Two Cs brawler with a holy excuse. A child of the Fire Circle who
speaks Hallit at home and Taldane in the street and lies in both. A brewery drayman, the most
respected trade in the city. A conscript caught stealing bread and put on a supply wagon, who
slipped away the moment it passed Drezen's gate. That is how **Tam**, a water-carrier in
Drezen, arrived. Asked about it, Tam said, "I was shrinkage."

## 4. Kenabres and the border: what fell, and what stands

**Kenabres** was the crusade's northern city: twelve thousand people on tiers rising from the
eastern plain, its western edge a cliff above the West Sellen. **Old Kenabres** at the top held
the oldest families and the administration, which were the same families; the **Ring District**
was the rest of the original town; **New Kenabres** was built for refugees of the lost
settlements, and the **Gate District** ten years later when New Kenabres overflowed, because the
settlements kept getting lost. The **Truestone Quarry**, ten miles east, sent stone weekly to
the walls. The road to Egede is 225 miles and ten days by caravan, past the **Chapel of the
Argent Shield**, with its chair.

It was also the crusade's conscience and its shame, which in Kenabres were the same building:
the seat of Hulrun's inquisition, where the generals **Dyre** and **Marcovina** fought the
Prelate for every soldier he wanted kept home to hunt heretics instead of demons. Heretics were
easier to find. The **Kite** held the first wardstone ever raised. The **Blackwing Library** held
the crusade's greatest store of learning on the demons and the planes. All of it was consulted.

**What is left.** Half the population, about six and a half thousand, in a city split by rifts,
its cathedral and the Kite destroyed, the library partly collapsed, and the Queen's own appointee
in the Prelate's chair. The **Defender's Heart**, the fortified inn where Irabeth held the
survivors together, is the heart of what remains. When the cathedral fell, the people went to the
pub, and the pub held. The chalkboard by its door has not been wiped since Arodus:

> *BEDS: none. SOUP: yes. WATER: boil it. MISSING: ask Anevia. FOUND: ask Anevia.
> MONGRELMEN ARE CUSTOMERS. Signed, I. Tirabade.*
>
> *(beneath, in another hand) she means it*

The assault tore open the tunnels under the city, and out of them came the mongrelmen of
**Neathholm**, a hamlet of a few dozen on an island in an underground lake, under its one-eyed
chief **Chusul**. They lived under the holiest city in the north for ninety years and the holiest
city never once looked down.

**The road north.** **Valas's Gift**, a village of a couple of hundred north of Kenabres, named
for a holy man and its red-water springs. The springs were red before the war. The **Vineyard
Hills Waystation**, a day's ride south-east, a crossroads outpost well behind the old wardstone
line, and attacked anyway. The **Estrovian Forest**, where few go willingly: hernes, stag-horned
things that were once trespassing huntsmen, cursed by druids who then died of their own curse;
witchwargs; a pack of owlbears that may be ghosts; taiga giants; and the werebear-kin, the only
guides who will take you through, and the only inhabitants who could eat you and generally
choose not to. **Clydwell Keep** and the named forts, at full strength, guarding the memory of a
line. And far off, the cursed ruins of **Icerift Castle**, about which people do not talk.

**Egede**, on the lake. The second city, nearly forty thousand, the end of the River Road and the
first Mendevian ground most crusaders set foot on. Where exhausted soldiers are sent to rest,
which means prayer at the temples or the covert brothels. The temples know about the brothels
and the brothels know about the temples and both keep the other's hours. Where merchants have
made an industry of bankrupting pilgrims on their way to the war, so that the demons get them
pre-stripped. Aroden drowned a demon lord here once, and Egedens celebrate **Drowning Day** for
twenty-four hours in a way outsiders find disturbing. They do not set a place for him. Egede has
always been practical.

**Bio hooks from Kenabres.** Where were you on Armasse? In the Gate District when the bridge came
down. In the quarry caravan, ten miles out, watching the sky do something the sky should not. In
the cells of the inquisition, which turned out to be the safest place in the city. In the
tunnels, and you did not know the tunnels were there. Among the refugees in the Defender's Heart
when everyone decided, on no evidence, that things were about to get better. A city watchman
with twelve years' service and a burn scar you explain differently every time. A carpenter who
lost workshop, tools and wife in an afternoon and has been very calm about it. A mongrelman of
Neathholm above ground for the first time, finding it overrated.

## 5. Old Sarkoris: the country that was

For a thousand years before the Worldwound, the land west of the Sellen was **Sarkoris**: a
savage, proud, painted Kellid kingdom of fiercely independent clans, founded in 3672 when the
tribes broke the frost giants of the Northmounds and took the Northmounds as a prize. Everything
on the crusade's charts west of the river is Sarkorian, because the crusade has never had
anything better to put in its place. You can conquer a country three times and still be using its
maps.

**The people.** **Kellids**: lean, dark-haired, light-brown-skinned, with dark eyes and scars
they are proud of. A Kellid without scars is thought to have lived too comfortably, and a Kellid
who has lived comfortably is asked at whose expense. They dress in leather, fur and hide, and
wear **zoic fetishes**, charms of twine, hair and stone that their druids enchant. They value
strength, bravery and cunning; they have no patience for compromise or weak leaders; they carry
only what the clan needs; they respect Ulfen and dwarves and distrust magic and machines, having
lived between Irrisen's witches and Numeria's engines and seen what each does to a neighbour.
The **clan** comes before the self. Names are short and percussive: **Dron**, **Gurog**,
**Kronug**; **Belka**, **Inkit**, **Yala**. Second names are for the distinguished; a clan name
matters more than either. Names of record run longer and stranger: **Uloric Dziergas** the
warlord, **Domora Hume** the god-caller, **Khraigorr Half-Face**, who got the name honestly, the
**Tarnshiavs** of Dyinglight, the **Widowknife** clanhold, the **Farheaven** clan.

**The clans and their rulers.** Sarkoris was never one thing, which is why it took the demons
almost forty years to finish it. Each clan-hold had its **clanliege**; the great cities had their
**witch-wardens**; warlords rose and fell. The mystics of Sarkoris warned, in the years before
4606, of ascendant chaos and a thinning between the worlds. They were right about everything. No
one listened, because Aroden was coming, and a place had been set.

**God-calling.** The heart of Sarkorian religion. A **god-caller** binds a spirit, the clan's own
patron, usually a native beast made strange, a bear with feathers, a wolf with wings, and calls
it into the world, where the clan worships it as their god. The god turns up. This is the whole of
the doctrine and the whole of the appeal. Southern churches call it the worship of false gods, on
the authority of true ones who have not been seen since 4606. Many of the clan-gods were
destroyed or driven off in the first years of the Wound. The demons could find them. A Sarkorian
of god-calling blood may carry a name for a god no one has seen in a hundred years, and a grudge
on its behalf.

**The old gods.** Besides the clan-spirits: **Pulura**, Mistress of the Stars and the northern
lights, honoured at **Dyinglight** with a ring of idols, and paired with **Kostchtchie** the demon
of cold as the two faces of winter; the **Green Faith**, born in the Forest of Stones, which is
now the Stonewilds; **Gorum**, whom Kellids insist was one of their own and who has not denied
it; **Desna**, whom they see not as an elf but as a Kellid woman on an elk who leads them to
game; **Erastil**; **Pharasma** at Raliscrad; and names now half-lost: **Alglenweis**, **Dolok
Darkfur**, **Kagia**, **Ristrentho**, the **Stag Mother**, **Sturovenen** the Dragoneagle. There
was worship of Sarenrae here too, in shrines like the one two days west of Drezen. And there was
always Deskari's cult in the Northmounds, driven out by Aroden and never quite gone. The thing
about locusts is that you never get all of them.

**Huzuris.** The clans kept the memories of their chiefs and witch-wardens in the skulls of
animals bound to the clan's story: the **huzuri**, in which the clan's soul was held. A clan that
lost its huzuri had lost itself. Some came out with the refugees. Most did not. By their own
theology, most of the clans of Sarkoris are dead, and the people walking around under clan names
are ghosts who have not been told. An old woman of the Fire Circle, asked about this by a
Pathfinder with a notebook, said: "We were told. We're just not stopping."

**The cities.** **Iz**, the capital, a centre of metalwork beside its mines, and the first to
fall; two-thirds of it went into the rift the day it opened, and the remaining third has been the
demons' capital ever since, with embassies. **Undarin**, on the Sarkora, the market where the
miners of the plateau met the herders of the steppe; the demons found the stockyards useful.
**Storasta**, where the Sarkora meets the West Sellen, a shaman-ruled city of groves and gardens;
the last to fall, its sacred circles held by witches and shamans with druids of Mendev and
Numeria beside them, and their last stand is the ***Song of Sarkoris***, which is very beautiful.
**Dyinglight**, in the fens of the far north-west, the spiritual heart of the kingdom; the
priests of **Pulura's Fall** received a vision and the city emptied before the demons arrived,
the single instance in this history of a god warning anyone in time. She is a minor one.
**Raliscrad**, the trade town where the Sarkora met the Isk, abandoned in such haste that the
wealth was left on the tables, where the demons found it. **Gundrun**, far to the south-west,
destroyed, then rebuilt around 4670 by people who simply came back, and who have survived by
being beneath notice. **Domora** in the west, where a god-caller named Hume called a water spirit
out of a pool in 4172 and taught the art anew; the pool is still there. **Threshold**, a name at
the lip of the canyon country, which the old accounts call a prison-tower where Sarkoris kept its
arcane casters under the eyes of the druids. And under the Wolfscrags, **Jormurdun**, a dwarven
Sky Citadel lost since 1600 AR, whose royal line scattered with the pieces of a broken key.

**The diaspora.** The refugees went everywhere, and everywhere had an opinion. Into Mendev, where
the Third Crusade's inquisitors treated their animism as demon-worship and burned them for it.
Into Numeria. Into Ustalav, where the locals call them **leechfolk** and where the northern
Shudderwood is haunted by Sarkorians the Ustalavs murdered, who have at least stayed to make a
point. West to the Mammoth Lords, who share their blood and took them in the way one takes in a
cousin. And a stubborn handful never left: the folk of Gundrun, the **Tarnshiavs** at Shadow
Spring in the fen, the Kellids of **Sabertooth Vale** (who are not what they were, and would
rather you did not ask), and the humans of **Raliscrad** who chose to work for a demon rather than
run. Three generations on, "Sarkorian" means a great-grandmother's stories, a word or two of
Hallit, a tattoo pattern, a skull in a box, and the certainty that the country over the river is
*yours* and was taken.

What a Fire Circle grandmother keeps, from an inventory a landlord's clerk once tried to make:

> *One (1) skull, elk, wrapped. Not for sale. Not for touching. Not for asking about.
> One (1) knife, the good one. One (1) knife, the other one.
> Four (4) words of Hallit the grandchildren know. Eleven (11) they do not.
> One (1) map of Undarin, drawn from memory, wrong.
> One (1) place set at every meal. Not for Aroden. For someone else. Don't ask about that either.*

**Bio hooks from Sarkoris.** A god-caller's granddaughter with the family god's name and no god,
which puts her ahead of most clerics. A huzuri-keeper whose clan is down to four people, three of
whom are not speaking. A Fire Circle cook who has never seen the country he calls home and cooks
it anyway. A Kellid raised by crusaders who burned her parents and taught her to be grateful. A
Sarkorian who came home with the supply wagons because the walls at Drezen are the first walls on
this side of the river a Kellid could sleep behind in seventy-five years. A crusader built them.
A demon kept them. She sleeps fine. Someone from Gundrun, who has lived his whole life inside the
Wound and wants to know what all the fuss is about.

## 6. The Worldwound: what Sarkoris became

A hundred and seven years ago a mile-long blight rimmed in black flame opened in the barrows of
the Northmounds, and the land came apart around it: a fan of canyons reaching nearly to every
border, rivers gone into their beds and staying there, and a fire that has not gone out since.
The ground itself shifts in front of you the nearer you go to the rift; at the edges it settles,
mostly, and the crusade has built its cities on the mostly. Ashstorms come off it. The sky over
the centre is poison and coloured lightning. Even the animals are tainted. Every crusader chart of
it is a copy of a Sarkorian chart, because the crusade has never held anything long enough to
name it.

The crusade's recruiters have a sheet for this, *Questions Commonly Asked by Those Considering
Service West of the Sellen*, the most honest document the crusade has ever printed, because it
was written by a clerk who had been there:

> *Is it as bad as they say?* Yes.
> *Is the ground safe to walk on?* Until it is not. You will not be warned.
> *What do the demons want?* To watch.
> *Are there people living there?* Yes. Do not assume they want rescuing. Some of them have
> jobs.
> *What is the pay?* The pay is discussed at the citadel.
> *What should I bring?* Boots. A second pair of boots. Something to be buried with, or a note
> saying you do not mind.

**The five regions**, as the crusade's scholars divide them, from a safe distance:

- **The Wounded Lands**: the east, and the rift itself, what was the Northmounds, now a canyon a
  mile wide and dozens of miles long, full of chaos-stuff and bubbling primordial coagulation.
  **Iz** sits at its heart and **Drezen** on its eastern arm. The crusade's forward citadel and
  the enemy's capital are on the same limb. The **Marchlands**, badlands, canyon and lava west of
  Drezen, are what the crusade calls the part of this it has had to learn.
- **The Stonewilds**: the broken high country to the north, once the **Forest of Stones**,
  birthplace of the Green Faith. When it fell, a faction of its druids chose undeath rather than
  defeat, and the **Stonewild Sect** now defends the Stonewilds against demon and living alike,
  having concluded that the living were the problem. The **Petrified Library** and **Greengrave
  Keep** are up there. Nobody in Drezen can say who holds either. Nobody in Drezen has
  volunteered.
- **Riftshadow**: the river country, the Sarkora and its towns, once the most populous part of
  the kingdom, now ruins under demonic barges. **Undarin** at its north end, **Storasta** at its
  south, **Raliscrad** between, and the river running past all three as if nothing had happened.
- **Frostmire**: the frozen north-west, the **Wolfscrags** with their packs of dire wolves, and
  **Frostmire Fen**, frigid marshland broken by sulphurous hot springs. Fiendish marsh giants hold
  Dyinglight and have taken up cannibalism, which the demons find a bit much; hezrou dredge the
  fen for relics; things called bowel worms live in the water, and the name is descriptive.
- **The Sarkorian Steppe**: the far west, open, flat, dry, crossed by mammoth and woolly
  rhinoceros and by the war-bands of the **Mammoth Lords**, who push the border east a mile at a
  time and lose it again, and have been doing so for a century with the patience of people who
  have never heard of a crusade. The **Shudderwood**, the one great forest left, fills its
  southern corner, and the fey there hate everyone now, evenly.

**The rivers.** The **West Sellen** is the eastern boundary and the road the crusade came in by.
The **Ahari** ran under Drezen's walls until the Wound opened; its dry bed now cuts the city in
half. The **Sarkora** still flows, out of habit; the **Rolling River** and the **Isk** do not,
and their dry beds are roads. The **Gray Road** west of Drezen is a dry riverbed named for what
walks it. The locals decline to be more specific.

**Who rules.** Nothing, and everything, and it is more stable than Mendev. A loose coalition of
demonic warlords, each holding a fief by force or by guile, united only in wanting Golarion to
suffer, which as a political programme has the merit of being achievable. The greatest is the
balor **Khorramzadeh**, the **Storm King**, who rules Iz and took Kenabres. Next was the marilith
**Aponavisius**, who took Drezen in 4638 and held it until Rova; she is not in it now, and is
somewhere. The most respected is the witch **Areelu Vorlesh**, who held Undarin and is said to
have had a hand in opening the Wound. Respected, note, not obeyed. Demons do not obey. They are
merely impressed. The lilitu **Minagho** kept Raliscrad, with a staff. Under them, the
**Templars of the Ivory Labyrinth**, Baphomet's secret society, whose cells hide inside
respectable families and who have infiltrated crusader orders before, and the cults of
**Deskari**, Lord of the Locust Host, whose green-and-red locust wings are the sign of the enemy,
and whose entire theology is that everything should be eaten. He has been at this since 4606. He
is not in a hurry. Nobody who is winning is.

**Humans in the Wound.** More than you would think, and fewer than there were. Cultists and
mercenaries, who will tell you their employers keep their word; chattel kept for labour, food or
sacrifice, in that order of preference; the willing workers of Raliscrad; the free folk of
Gundrun; the enslaved miners of Iz, who broke every production record under the whip; and the
thousands who lived in occupied Drezen. A character born inside the Wound is possible. Ask the
DM. Ask nicely.

**What you meet on the road.** Babau, brimoraks, vrocks, nabasu, shadow demons; derakni in the
sky; vescavors, colony-swarms that eat metal and skin and are not particular about the order;
plague steeds; urannags; the fungus called Shax's beard, which grows where it likes; ghouls in the
chapels; the winter wraiths of the Crown of the World, raised from Drezen's own dead and still,
in some sense, on the garrison rolls. And seventy-five years of names on the charts, every one of
them marking a place someone was sure was safe.

## 7. Drezen: the city you are standing in

**What it was.** A citadel-city raised in the hubris of the First Crusade, on the eastern arm of
the Wounded Lands, a monument to the proposition that the war was as good as won. In 4638 it was
betrayed from within by **Staunton Vhane**, a dwarf paladin turned, and taken by
**Aponavisius**; a wave of raw chaos turned most of its people into beast-things that ate each
other, and the corpses were carted to the ice cliffs of the Crown of the World, where they rose
as winter wraiths. Drezen's dead were put to work before they were cold. **Saint Argil**, the
Pauper Sorcerer, died on the walls covering the flight of those who got out, and was made a saint
for it, which is the crusade's pension scheme. The shrines of Iomedae and Sarenrae were made into
mockeries of themselves, by demons who had clearly studied the originals. For seventy-five years,
three generations, it was a demon city of some seven thousand.

**How it was taken.** By a hundred volunteers and the four champions, in one week of Rova: the
courtyard on the 8th, the sally port and the Templar's Court on the 9th, three days on the first
floor of the citadel, Staunton Vhane dead and buried on the 12th and his glaive unmade in the
forge, and the dungeons on the 13th, where the banner was. The enemy's catapults were wrecked
during the siege, which nobody had thought to do in seventy-five years. **Kamilo Dann**, who held
a dozen wounded in a stone gaol at Vilareth Ford until the ford was retaken, is one of many small
heroes of that fortnight, and yours can be another. Small is a description of the pay.

**Who runs it now.** In the sense that anyone does.

- **Irabeth Tirabade**, half-orc paladin of the Eagle Watch, Governor. Held Kenabres together
  with her bare hands and was released by the Queen to ride north. On being handed Drezen: "I
  had a city. It fell on me. Give me another."
- **Aron Kir**, former spy, now Warden; keeps the citadel's watch and its secrets, and has the
  look of a man who has read your file. He has read your file.
- **Horgus Gwerm**, a Kenabres noble who counted his gold in the rubble and now counts the
  city's grain. His office door reads: *NO. Come in and I will explain why.*
- **Anevia Tirabade**, the Governor's wife and the crusade's agent; her spies map the west.
- **Joran Vhane**, Staunton's kinsman, a dwarven master smith, surrendered in the citadel and
  now a penitent who dreams of relighting the forge beneath Drezen for good ends. Asked whether
  that was not what the last Vhane said, he answered, "He said it better. I'm going to do it."

For new arrivals, the Warden's office issues a single sheet:

> *ON ARRIVAL IN DREZEN*
> *Where do I sleep?* Where you can. The barracks are full. The inns are full. The temple
> compound has an orphanage, a food bank and an infirmary; qualify for one.
> *Where do I eat?* Rabiah's compound, the kitchen. Do not comment on the angle.
> *Where do I get paid?* The citadel, on completion, on presentation of the work, on the
> Warden's word that the work was done. The Warden's word is not for sale. Do not ask what it is
> for.
> *Is the South Bank safe?* The South Bank is across the river.
> *Is the river safe?* The river is dry.
> *Is the dry river safe?* Use the bridges.
> *Who do I report a demon to?* Anyone. Loudly.
> *Who do I report a crusader to?* The Warden. Quietly.
> *Where is the Queen's steward?* Nerosyan. He was here for an afternoon. We have his chair.

**The lie of the city.** The citadel on the high ground, the Preservers' compound north-east of
it, the temple compound and Rabiah's crooked ring quarter on the eastern ridge, the Celeste
Market packed between the citadel wall and the dry riverbed, and across the riverbed the **South
Bank**, scrap yards and rope-walks that spent three generations under demon rule and have not
been searched. **Cinder Row** runs through it. That is where the work is. The rest is on the Map
tab, and the map is in this document's voice, so you have been warned.

**The roads.** East and south, the road down to **Kenabres**: **Keeper's Canyon**, where two
armies camped, one of which is still there in pieces; **Vilareth Ford**; about a week's march
for a column, less for a rider. West, the road to the **Hidden Temple**: two days on foot, with
nothing on it. "Nothing" is a technical term. Beyond that, the **Gray Road**, the **Weeping
Hills**, the lava country, which the company will not be asked to visit, until it is. North,
the **Lake Lost to the Sun**, named for the light that does not reach it. A hex on the
Marchlands chart is twelve miles, or a hard day's march.

**What Drezen feels like.** A garrison learning to be a town, and the town learning what a
garrison thinks a town is for. Acid-scarred stone. Two temples facing each other across a
street, both newer than the season. More buildings than people, and a standing policy of not
finding out what is in them. Every party that goes west leaves by the western gate and is
counted home through the same arch. The ledger at the gate has three columns: *Out*, *In*, and
*Shrinkage*. It is the most complete record Drezen keeps.

## 8. Building the bio: five questions

You do not need a life story. You need enough that the DM can hurt you with it. Answer these,
briefly, and stop.

**Where are you from?** Pick a place from §2 through §7, or one that is not here and should be.
A quarter of a city, a village on a road, a cave, a boat. Then: who is still there, and do they
know where you are? A Fire Circle grandmother, a quarry foreman, a clan of four, a landlord. One
person you would go back for, or one you are making sure never finds you.

**What are you good at, and why?** Not the class. The thing the class grew out of. You can climb
because the Gate District had no stairs to your floor. You can read because the inquisition
taught you, and you have thought about that since. You can lie in two languages. You can carry
a wounded man a mile because you have. Say where you learned it and who paid for the lesson.

**What are you afraid of, and why?** Everyone in Mendev is afraid of the same three things and
has a fourth of their own. The fourth is the useful one. It can be small. Fire. Bells. Being
counted. Being the twelfth on a wagon that arrives with nine. Something that happened on Armasse
that you have not told anyone, and a country with one story does not need to hear yours to guess
it.

**Why are you in Drezen right now?** Not why you came. Why you are still here, this week, when
the road south is open and nobody checks papers. The pay. A wall. A name. A grave. A god. A debt.
Somebody in the *In* column you are waiting for. A reason you have told no one and the Warden
has on file already. Pick one and be honest about whether it is a good one.

**Where do you see yourself in five levels?** This is the question nobody in Drezen asks,
because the answer is the cemetery, which is the only district with a growing population. Ask it
anyway. A stall in the Celeste Market. A squad of your own. The forge relit. The skull carried
home to a river that runs again. Out of the *Shrinkage* column for good. The ballad is taken; a
verse in it is not. Write down what your character would want if wanting were allowed here, and
let the DM decide what it costs.

Then two smaller things. **What do you carry** that is not on the equipment list: a zoic fetish
that protected you from everything except what happened; a bowl from Tuesday's mistake you were
meant to return; a sword-and-sun badge on a coat that was somebody else's; a brewery token, the
one currency that has held its value. And **what is the gesture you make** when you pass the
temples. Do it now. Think about what it does.

### Naming by people

- **Native Mendevian**: Iobarian roots, sturdy and slightly Slavic-sounding, the names of people
  who expected to be here a while. *Hulrun Shappok, Irabeth Tirabade, Anevia, Sosiel Vaenic,
  Nestrin Alodae, Kamilo Dann, Trudean Delashaw, Jeskar Hinton, Tilda Merrow.*
- **Crusader stock**: southern names from anywhere: Taldan (*Galfrey, Aron Kir, Aldrich Thorne,
  Brannic Vayne, Dyre, Marcovina*), Chelaxian, Andoren, Varisian. A family name that is a place
  ("of Lauterbury") is a knightly affectation, and usually a place that has never heard of them.
- **Sarkorian / Kellid**: short and hard: *Dron, Gurog, Kronug, Belka, Inkit, Yala*; the clan
  name over the given name; longer and stranger for the notable: *Uloric Dziergas, Domora Hume,
  Khraigorr Half-Face, Tarnshiav, Widowknife, Farheaven.* Nobody in Sarkoris was called
  something they could not shout across a valley.
- **Mongrelmen of Neathholm**: single, plain, given underground by people who did not expect
  anyone above to ask: *Lann, Chusul, Opoli, Chyrrik.*
- **Drezen-born**: whatever the demons let them be called. *Fenna Tusk* is the one example, and
  she seems to have chosen it herself.
