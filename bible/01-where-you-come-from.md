# Where You Come From: A Character Bio Helper

*The Real Work. The lands around Drezen, for anyone writing a character who is standing in the
city in the autumn of 4713 AR and has not yet worked out why.*

This is a handout for building a backstory. It is not a chapter, not a rulebook, and not a
promise that any of it will still be true by Kuthona. It gathers what passes for fact about the
country around **Drezen** (the Worldwound, old Sarkoris, Mendev and its capital Nerosyan) from
Paizo's published setting and from the crusade's own chronicle at
[The Fifth Crusade](https://mattdahse.github.io/the-fifth-crusade/), and it stops at the present
day.

**Which source wins.** Where Paizo and the chronicle differ, the chronicle governs at this table:
its spellings, its dates, its people. Where the chronicle is silent, Paizo's setting stands. Where
both are silent, make something up and tell Matt, so that it can be written down and later
contradicted by a dice roll.

**How to use it.** Skim §1 so you know what everyone in the city knows. Then read the one or two
sections that match where your character is from, and pick from the prompts in §8.

**The general situation, for those in a hurry.** A god was due back in 4606 to settle everything.
He died instead. A hole opened where he was supposed to stand, and everything since has been
people building walls in front of the hole and demons taking the walls down, for a hundred and
seven years, on both sides with great sincerity. The demons are enjoying it more. You have been
hired to do the part of this that does not fit in a song.

## 1. The moment you are standing in

It is **Neth, 4713 AR**. Late autumn, with the first hard frosts on the Marchlands and the
southern horizon lit orange after dark. Everyone in Drezen, whoever they are, carries these
facts, mostly in the way one carries a bad tooth.

**Kenabres fell on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus.** Iomedae's own feast day. The Storm King came
over the walls, the silver dragon **Terendelev** died in the sky above the city, the great bridge
went down, and the wardstone in the Kite was shattered. The city that had been the crusade's
northern wall for a century fell in an afternoon. Prelate **Hulrun** had spent fifty years
burning people to keep the demons out. The demons killed him first.

**The wardstones are gone.** A week later, at the **Gray Garrison**, four nobodies out of the
rubble broke the corrupted fragment before the enemy could turn it against the army gathering at
the border, and every wardstone along the West Sellen went dark with it. The border that held for
seventy-five years is now a river. The power the stones held went into the four nobodies. The
church has ruled this a miracle. The Riftwardens have asked to see the paperwork.

**The four** are **Rabiah**, a red-haired sorcerer of sixteen who keeps herself spotless in a
country made of ash; **Harlock Greyforge**, a half-orc of Iomedae who carries a sword that talks,
and listens to it; **Lupenor Celest**, an elven archer of Desna; and **Varic Sarian**, a half-elf
priest of the Dawnflower who has taken up hitting things. The table calls them **Rabiah's
Redeemers**. Mendev calls them the Wardstone's Champions, and there is a ballad. You will hear
it. You will hear it in every tavern between here and Egede, sung by people who could not find
Drezen on a chart, and the verse about the forty at the door gets longer every month.

**Queen Galfrey rode north** to Kenabres herself, and sent the four to Drezen with a hundred
volunteers. They marched out on the 30th of Arodus, took **Vilareth Ford** back on the road,
declined to walk into **Keeper's Canyon** in the dark, and came within sight of Drezen's walls on
the 6th of Rova. Most of the hundred are alive. Nobody had planned for that, and the barracks are
short of beds.

**Drezen changed hands on Fire Day, the 13th of Rova, 4713.** The citadel that fell to the demons
in 4638 is a crusader city again. The ***Sword of Valor***, the banner lost when it fell, was
carried out of the dungeons beneath it, flawless after seventy-five years in demon hands.
**Staunton Vhane**, the dwarf who betrayed the city three generations ago, is buried here. The
banner came through the occupation without a stain. Nothing else did.

**Sir Aldrich Thorne** came from the Queen the next day, by teleportation, with two guards. He
saw the city passed to **Irabeth Tirabade** to govern, declined a meal, and left the same
afternoon. He has not been back. Ask anyone in Drezen what the Queen's man thought of the place
and they will tell you how long he stayed.

**The four are away in the west**, days out of contact, and what they are doing there is not
common knowledge. The cohorts they left behind are holding the city on a thread, and hiring. You
are the thread.

If your character has been in Drezen more than a week, they have also heard: that there is a
thousand-platinum bounty posted by the demons themselves on a succubus somewhere in the west,
which is more than the crusade has ever offered for anything; that a shrine of Sarenrae two days
down the western road has been reopened and is being kept by a priest named **Rothin Vald**;
that the **South Bank** has not been searched; and that **Horgus Gwerm**, the quartermaster, is
browbeating the crown for grain. His last dispatch to Nerosyan is public knowledge because he
read it aloud in the market first:

> *Your Majesty. Drezen holds. Drezen has four hundred mouths more than it had on the 13th and
> the same grain. I am told the crown's stores are committed to Kenabres. I am told a great many
> things. Send wheat, or send fewer people, or send Sir Aldrich back to explain it to them; he
> will not need long.*
>
> *Gwerm, Quartermaster.*

## 2. Mendev: the crusader state

**The land.** Mendev is the northern end of the continent: snow-covered mountains, icy steppe,
and the great evergreen **Estrovian Forest** across its centre and south. North, the ice cliffs
of the Crown of the World. East, across the forest, the **Lake of Mists and Veils**, which every
crusader crossed to get here. South, across the rivers, **Numeria**, a harsh country of savages
and alien metal that Mendevians want nothing to do with. West, the **West Sellen River**, and on
its far bank the Worldwound. There is almost no arable land. The country imports nearly
everything, and for a century the crusade has been fed by boats: crusaders and grain coming up
the Sellen and across the lake, arriving weekly at **Egede**. The grain is inspected. The
crusaders are not.

**Three kinds of Mendevian.** Nobody in Mendev is just "a Mendevian." Ask which.

- **The natives.** Descended from Iobarian exiles, semi-nomadic once, and culturally cousins to
  the Sarkorians across the river. Many still keep the old **animist** faith or the **Green
  Faith**, quietly. The crusaders have treated them as second-class for a hundred years and in
  the Third Crusade burned them for heresy. A native Mendevian character has a grandmother who
  remembers who informed on whom. The crown kept no such list. The grandmothers did.
- **The crusaders and their children.** From Cheliax, Isger, Andoran, Taldor and everywhere
  else. The first wave were the finest people of the south: clerics, paladins, knights who sold
  their lands to come. By the Fourth Crusade the boats were bringing mercenaries, dispossessed
  nobles, wandering companies, political undesirables and men let out of prison on condition they
  went north, and by now these outnumber the pious. A crusader's-child character can be
  third-generation Mendevian and still be called a foreigner by the natives, whose great-uncle
  their grandfather may have denounced.
- **The Sarkorians.** Kellid refugees of the lost kingdom, three generations in exile. See §5.
  In Nerosyan they have a quarter of their own along the western wall, the **Fire Circle**, with
  shops and lodgings kept in the old style. Others took the caves of **Saresp Mountain** in the
  far east, or scattered into Numeria, Ustalav and beyond.

And among them: **tieflings** in unusual numbers (Mendev is home to many asura-spawn, and the
Wound breeds pitborn); **dwarves** whose ancestors fled the lost Sky Citadel of **Jormurdun**
with a royal claim and a piece of a broken key, and who mostly live poor in the Fleshward slum of
Chesed on the lake, still holding the piece; halflings; half-elves; and beneath Kenabres, the
**mongrelmen**, the warped descendants of First Crusade soldiers whom the Wound changed and whom
their own comrades then tried to kill.

**Rule.** Mendev is a monarchy under **Queen Galfrey**, the Sword of Iomedae, a Taldan woman who
looks to be in her middle forties and is over a hundred years old, held there by the sun orchid
elixir. She was crowned in 4601 as a paladin of Aroden; five years later Aroden died and the
Worldwound opened, and she retrained herself as Iomedae's paladin with the brisk practicality of
a woman whose first god had let her down. She is the one who ordered the retreat from Sarkoris in
4638 so that the wardstones could be raised, and she has been hated for it ever since by the
descendants of the people it kept alive. Her word is law within Nerosyan's walls. Beyond them,
every commander of every city, fort and army interprets her commands a little differently. Until
this summer the second most powerful person in the kingdom was **Prelate Hulrun Shappok** of
Kenabres, the old inquisitor whose witch-burnings as a young man started the Third Crusade, and
who only in his last years began to understand the shame he had brought on the faith. He was
working on it.

**Faith.** **Iomedae**, the Inheritor, is the crusade. Her white-and-gold banner is the one all
crusaders nominally fight under, her cathedral is the centre of Nerosyan, and her holy day
**Armasse** is the day Kenabres fell. She inherited her portfolio from Aroden, who died on the
day he was due to return. The Chapel of the Argent Shield on the Egede road, once his, still
keeps a chair for him by the altar. Nobody sits in it. Nobody has moved it either. Beside her:
the remnants of Aroden's church, who began the First Crusade; **Sarenrae**, whose priests march
with the columns and heal what the columns leave; **Shelyn**, **Desna**, **Erastil**, **Abadar**,
**Pharasma**. **Gorum** has grown popular in the barracks of Egede, and **Cayden Cailean**'s
faithful have a whole neighbourhood in Nerosyan with the city's best-attended services. The
Green Faith and the animist ways of the natives survive, and are looked at sideways. Every
Mendevian, pious or not, knows the gesture: fingers to the lips, then pressed to the
sword-pommel, as one crosses oneself before something sacred. Ask a Mendevian what it does.
Watch them do it again while they think.

Three sayings you will hear in any Mendevian kitchen:

> *Aroden is coming. Set a place.*
> *The wall is only as good as the priest is sober.*
> *Pray to Iomedae. Pay Abadar. Drink with Cayden. Bury with Pharasma. Ask the trees when nobody's looking.*

**Orders and outfits.** A crusader belongs to something, or pretends to.

- **The Eagle Watch**: the crusaders of Kenabres. **Irabeth Tirabade** commanded them at the
  Defender's Heart; **Anevia**, her wife, served among them; Harlock was on the eve of his oath
  to them when the city fell.
- **The Order of Heralds**: the knightly order that curbed the Third Crusade's purges and turned
  the war back outward. Still the conscience of the crusade. See the table above for their
  reward.
- **The Riftwardens**: a secretive society of planar scholars who want the Worldwound *closed*,
  not merely held, based in Kenabres's **Librarium of the Broken Black Wing**, which has since
  partly collapsed. **Aravashnial**, the elven wizard blinded in the fall of Kenabres and since
  healed by the Queen's own priests, is one. Their mark is the Seeker's Spiral, and it is not a
  thing to give a new character without asking.
- **Iomedae's Preservers**: the newest of the orders, founded this autumn by **Harlock
  Greyforge** and headquartered in Drezen. Five squads of paladins and clerics sworn to hold the
  city. Their recruiting bill is nailed to the citadel gate:
  > *IOMEDAE'S PRESERVERS seek the steadfast. Three days' trial. Bring your own boots. Food
  > provided on the third day. Those who leave before the third day are asked to return the
  > boots if they were not their own. Apply to Dame Elara Dawnstrider, who will know if you are
  > lying.*
- **The Kenabres Wolves**: the spies and scouts, **Aron Kir**'s people. He is Warden of Drezen
  now.
- **The Order of Holy Sentinels**: guardians of the ***Demonscope*** at **Clydwell Keep**, the
  greatest of the border forts. Every fort on the old wardstone line is named for a dead general.
- **The knightly orders**: the Emerald Sword, the Flaming Lance, the Rampant Dragon, the Sunrise
  Sword, the Guild of Diggers and Sappers, and dozens more. Some are ancient. Some were invented
  on the boat, because the heralds of Iomedae cannot check every newcomer's claim to a title.
  The heralds know this. The knights know the heralds know. The titles are used anyway. A title
  is a lie everyone has agreed on, and Mendev has agreed on a great many.
- **The Low Templars**: the crusade coined the name for its mercenaries, sellswords and
  adventurers, then noticed it had coined a name for most of the crusade, and kept it.
- **The Pathfinder Society**: present in Nerosyan since the Queen gave them the Starrise Spire
  in 4707, and sending expeditions into the Wound disguised as mercenary companies.
- **The inquisitors**: Hulrun's people. Fewer than they were, and quieter since Kenabres.

**Banners.** Any crusader stronghold is a riot of colour: streamers and flags from hundreds of
clans, counties, kingdoms and families competing for room. The banners that matter are the ones
earned on the field, and they take the form of Iomedae's own symbols, the **sword**, the
**shield**, or the **sunburst**, granted to soldiers of any rank. It is the one honour in Mendev
that cannot be bought, and it is a piece of cloth.

**Speech.** Mendevians speak **Taldane** (Common); the natives and the Sarkorians also speak
**Hallit**, the Kellid tongue, which has no written form. Two words of Mendevian slang worth
knowing: a **gaffle** or a **rip** is a scam. There is no Mendevian word for an honest bargain.
They use the Taldane, and they use it carefully.

**The wardstones, and their absence.** For seventy-five years the border was a line of
rune-carved menhirs along the West Sellen and the Moutray, infused with **nexovar** from the
hills of the Egelsee, maintained by daily prayer and a yearly ritual, and housed in a chain of
forts. Nothing chaotic or evil could pass between them, and no teleportation could cross the
line. They are gone since Arodus. The forts are still garrisoned. The prayers are still said,
every morning, over stones that no longer hear them. A character who served on the wardstone line
has lost the whole meaning of their posting and kept the posting. The pay still comes.

## 3. Nerosyan: the Diamond of the North

The capital, and the crusade's fortress. Before the Worldwound it was a run-down river town of
pirates; the First Crusade widened its streets, walled it, filled it with soldiers and gave it a
holy purpose, and the pirates' grandchildren are now the city guard. Population about sixty-five
thousand, nearly all human.

**Where it sits.** At the junction where the **Egelsee River** runs into the northernmost branch
of the **West Sellen**. The Egelsee is the city's only clean water; the Sellen comes down too
close to the Wound to drink. Everything outside the walls has been burned flat, by demonic raids
and by the defenders, so that nothing approaches unseen. Nobody now remembers which fires were
whose.

**How it is built.** A diamond between the two rivers, so that every wall has water in front of
it. The outer walls are twenty feet thick, crenellated and towered, and the wall-tops are
*roofed*: sloped roofs cut with arrow slits, because the enemy flies. At the centre stands the
**Cruciform Cathedral** of Iomedae, and the avenues run wide and straight from it so that a
sortie can reach any wall in minutes, and so that the cathedral can see every door. Four great
towers hold the points: the **Battle Tower** in the north, the most heavily fortified structure
in Mendev; the **Woundward Tower**, which holds the city's wardstone and its best archers and
engines against attack from the air; the **Confluence Tower**, guarding the rivers and sheltering
Crusader's Quay; and the **Egelsee Tower**, the cavalry's staging ground. The **Sellen Docks**
are stone quays cut into the bedrock. Above it all burns the **Starrise Spire**, older than the
walls, with a ball of light floating over it that has not gone out in centuries: a beacon to the
crusade and a warning to the Wound, and the Wound has had a century to get used to it.

**The districts.** Battle, Egelsee, Confluence and Woundward are the four quarters. Within them:
**Pilgrim's Rest**, where the endless stream of Iomedaean pilgrims lodges; **Bannerbright**,
where the knightly and clerical orders keep their houses and their feuds; the **Two Cs**, the
neighbourhood of Cayden Cailean's faithful, which is where the taverns are; the **Fire Circle**
along the western wall, where the Sarkorian refugees keep their shops and eating houses in the
old style, facing the wall, facing home. On the east side, **Hank the Alerunner's** hideout is a
tavern full of relics of the city's pirate days. **Ancuin's Brewery** has been the crusaders'
favourite for a hundred years and is the institution with the best record in Mendev.

From the pamphlet handed to pilgrims at the Sellen Docks, in its fourth printing:

> *WELCOME TO NEROSYAN, DIAMOND OF THE NORTH. Pilgrims will find lodging in Pilgrim's Rest, at
> rates set by the crown. Pilgrims will find other lodging in the Two Cs, at rates set by the
> Two Cs. The Cathedral is open at all hours. The Woundward Tower is not a viewing platform.
> Please do not feed the Sarkorians; they have their own quarter and their own food and they
> would like you to know that. Recruiters are stationed at every gate and are not permitted to
> follow you into the Cathedral. They know this. Walk quickly.*

**Who runs it.** The Queen, from her palace, counselled by an angel named **Opaline**, who has
presumably seen the whole plan and is not sharing. Around her: **Lord Inquisitor Casori**;
**Captain Jaspar** of the Crusader Heralds; Venture-Captain **Jorsal of Lauterbury** of the
Pathfinders. The city is law-abiding, beautiful in its design, and entirely clear about what it
is for.

**What has happened lately.** For years the city has been harried by things that come up the
rivers: merrow, scrags, grindylows, the water-things that serve the hezrou that rules ruined
Storasta downriver. There are stories of a larger siege this year, turned back with the
Pathfinders in the thick of it, which the Pathfinders tell. The wagons of conscripts and supply
have been rolling north to Kenabres and on to Drezen since Rova. The wagons leave Nerosyan with
twelve conscripts and reach Drezen with nine. The quartermaster's ledger records this as
*shrinkage*.

**Bio hooks from Nerosyan.** A dock-hand on the Sellen quays who has unloaded a hundred
boatloads of heroes and watched none come back down. A cathedral acolyte who has never been
outside the walls and believes everything, so far. A Two Cs tavern brawler with a holy excuse. A
child of the Fire Circle who speaks Hallit at home and Taldane in the street and lies in both. A
brewery drayman, the most respected trade in the city. A conscript caught stealing bread and put
on a supply wagon, who slipped away the moment it passed Drezen's gate. That is how **Tam**, the
Redeemers' water-carrier, arrived. Asked about it, Tam said, "I was shrinkage." A clerk in the
palace who saw the Queen's letter from Drezen before it was read aloud, and saw what was cut.

## 4. Kenabres and the border: what fell, and what stands

**Kenabres** was the crusade's northern city: twelve thousand people on graduated tiers rising
from the eastern plain, its western edge a cliff above the West Sellen, its water drawn from the
river below. **Old Kenabres** at the top held the oldest families and the administration, which
were the same families; the **Ring District** around it was the rest of the original town; **New
Kenabres** was built after the Worldwound opened, for refugees of the lost settlements, and the
**Gate District** ten years later when New Kenabres overflowed, because the settlements kept
getting lost. The **Truestone Quarry**, ten miles east, sent stone weekly to the Southgate to
reinforce the walls. The road to Egede is 225 miles and ten days by caravan, skirting the
Estrovian Forest past the **Chapel of the Argent Shield**, with its chair.

It was also the crusade's conscience and its shame, which in Kenabres were the same building:
the seat of Hulrun's inquisition, where the Third Crusade's fires burned hottest, and where the
generals **Dyre** and **Marcovina** fought the Prelate for every soldier he wanted kept home to
hunt heretics instead of demons. Heretics were easier to find. The **Kite** held the first
wardstone ever raised. The **Cathedral of Saint Clydwell** stood in the old town. The
**Blackwing Library** held the crusade's greatest store of learning on the demons and the planes.
All of it was consulted.

**What is left.** Half the population, about six and a half thousand, in a city split by rifts,
its cathedral and the Kite destroyed, the library partly collapsed, and the Queen's own appointee
in the Prelate's chair. The **Defender's Heart**, the fortified inn where Irabeth held the
survivors together, is the heart of what remains. When the cathedral fell, the people went to the
pub, and the pub held. The chalkboard by its door has not been wiped since Arodus:

> *BEDS: none. SOUP: yes. WATER: boil it. MISSING: ask Anevia. FOUND: ask Anevia.
> MONGRELMEN ARE CUSTOMERS. Signed, I. Tirabade.*
>
> *(beneath, in another hand) she means it*

The assault tore open layers of tunnels under the city, and out of them came the mongrelmen of
**Neathholm**, a hamlet of a few dozen on an island in an underground lake, under its one-eyed
chief **Chusul**, descended from First Crusade soldiers whom the Wound changed and whom the
crusade then tried to kill. They lived under the holiest city in the north for ninety years and
the holiest city never once looked down. **Chyrrik**, who marched with the four, is one of them.

**The road north.** **Valas's Gift**, a village of a couple of hundred on the road north of
Kenabres, named for a holy man and its red-water springs. The springs were red before the war.
The **Vineyard Hills Waystation**, a day's ride south-east of the city, a crossroads outpost well
behind the old wardstone line, the last ordinary place the four stood before the march, and it
was attacked anyway. The **Estrovian Forest** itself, where few go willingly: hernes, stag-horned
things that were once trespassing huntsmen, cursed by druids who then died of their own curse;
witchwargs; a pack of owlbears that may be ghosts; taiga giants driven in by the opening of the
Wound; and the werebear-kin, respected as the only guides who will take you through, and as the
only inhabitants who could eat you and generally choose not to. **Clydwell Keep** and the named
forts of the wardstone line, at full strength, guarding the memory of a line. And far off, the
cursed ruins of **Icerift Castle**, about which people do not talk.

**Egede**, on the lake. The second city, nearly forty thousand, the end of the River Road and the
first Mendevian ground most crusaders ever set foot on. Where exhausted soldiers are sent to
rest, which means prayer at the temples or the covert brothels. The temples know about the
brothels and the brothels know about the temples and both keep the other's hours. Where merchants
have made an industry of bankrupting pilgrims on their way to the war, so that the demons get
them pre-stripped. Aroden drowned a demon lord here once, and Egedens celebrate **Drowning Day**
for twenty-four hours in a way outsiders find disturbing. They do not set a place for him. Egede
has always been practical.

**Bio hooks from Kenabres.** Where were you on Armasse? In the Gate District when the bridge came
down. In the quarry caravan, ten miles out, watching the sky do something the sky should not. In
the cells of the inquisition, which turned out to be the safest place in the city. In the
tunnels, and you did not know the tunnels were there, and neither did the people who built the
city on top of them. Among the refugees in the Defender's Heart when four filthy strangers
walked back in with the wardstone broken behind them and everyone decided, on no evidence, that
this was good. A city watchman with twelve years' service and a burn scar you explain
differently every time (that is **Sera Vost**; there are others like her). A craftsman's son who
followed Harlock because Harlock was walking in a direction (that is **Deren Ashfall**; there are
others like him). A carpenter who lost workshop, tools and wife in an afternoon and has been very
calm about it (that is **Tobias Runt**). A mongrelman of Neathholm above ground for the first
time, finding it overrated.

## 5. Old Sarkoris: the country that was

For a thousand years before the Worldwound, the land west of the Sellen was **Sarkoris**: a
savage, proud, painted Kellid kingdom of fiercely independent clans, founded in 3672 when a
confederation of tribes broke the frost giants of the Northmounds and took the Northmounds as a
prize. Everything on the crusade's charts west of the river is Sarkorian, the steadings, the
shrines, the holdfasts, because the crusade has never had anything better to put in their place.
You can conquer a country three times and still be using its maps.

**The people.** **Kellids**: lean, dark-haired, light-brown-skinned, with dark eyes and scars
they are proud of. A Kellid without scars is thought to have lived too comfortably, and a Kellid
who has lived comfortably is asked at whose expense. They dress in leather, fur and hide, and
wear **zoic fetishes**, little charms of twine, hair and stone that their druids enchant. They
value strength, bravery and cunning; they have little patience for compromise, carelessness or
weak leaders; they carry only what the clan needs; they respect Ulfen and dwarves as kindred
spirits and distrust magic and machines both, having lived between Irrisen's witches and
Numeria's engines and seen what each does to a neighbour. The **clan** comes before the self.
Names are short and percussive, two syllables or fewer: **Dron**, **Gurog**, **Kronug**;
**Belka**, **Inkit**, **Yala**. Second names are rare, kept for the distinguished or the
well-born; a clan name matters more than either. Sarkorian names of record run longer and
stranger: **Uloric Dziergas** the warlord, **Domora Hume** the god-caller, **Khraigorr
Half-Face**, who got the name honestly, the **Tarnshiavs** of Dyinglight, the **Widowknife**
clanhold, the **Farheaven** clan.

**The clans and their rulers.** Sarkoris was never one thing, which is why it took the demons
almost forty years to finish it. Each clan-hold had its **clanliege**; the great cities had their
**witch-wardens**; and warlords rose and fell. The mystics and witches of Sarkoris were a byword
across Avistan, and it was their oration that warned, in the years before 4606, of ascendant
chaos and a thinning between the worlds. They were right about everything. No one listened,
because Aroden was coming, and a place had been set.

**God-calling.** The heart of Sarkorian religion. A **god-caller** binds a spirit, the clan's own
patron, usually in the shape of a native beast made strange, a bear with feathers, a wolf with
wings, and calls it into the world, where the clan worships it as their god. The god turns up.
This is the whole of the doctrine and the whole of the appeal. The forms were consistent across
generations; marriages between clans produced hybrid gods. Southern churches call this the
worship of false gods, on the authority of true ones who have not been seen since 4606. Many of
the clan-gods were destroyed or driven off in the first years of the Wound. The demons could
find them. A Sarkorian character of god-calling blood may carry a name for a god no one has seen
in a hundred years, and a grudge on its behalf.

**The old gods.** Besides the clan-spirits: **Pulura**, the Shimmering Maiden, Mistress of the
Stars and the northern lights, honoured at **Dyinglight** with a great ring of idols, and paired
with **Kostchtchie** the demon of cold as the two faces of winter; the **Green Faith**, born in
the Forest of Stones, which is now the Stonewilds; **Gorum**, whom Kellids insist was one of
their own and who has not denied it; **Desna**, whom they see not as an elf but as a Kellid woman
on an elk who leads them to game; **Erastil**; **Pharasma** at Raliscrad, whose crypts are the
only thing in Raliscrad still in order; and names now half-lost: **Alglenweis**, **Dolok
Darkfur**, **Kagia**, **Ristrentho**, the **Stag Mother** of the Forest of Stones, **Sturovenen**
the Dragoneagle and his dawn-feathered children. There was worship of Sarenrae here too, in the
old days, in shrines like the one two days west of Drezen; **Brix Copperfinch**, a gnome scholar
of the Dawnflower, has made a life's study of it. And there was always Deskari's cult in the
Northmounds, driven out by Aroden and never quite gone. The thing about locusts is that you never
get all of them.

**Huzuris.** The clans preserved the memories of their chiefs and witch-wardens in the skulls of
animals bound to the clan's story: the **huzuri**, in which the clan's soul was held. A clan that
lost its huzuri had lost itself. Some came out with the refugees. Most did not. By their own
theology, most of the clans of Sarkoris are dead, and the people walking around under clan names
are ghosts who have not been told. An old woman of the Fire Circle, asked about this by a
Pathfinder with a notebook, said: "We were told. We're just not stopping."

**The cities.** **Iz**, the capital, a centre of stone and metalwork beside its mines, and the
first to fall; two-thirds of it went into the rift the day it opened, and the remaining third has
been the demons' capital ever since, with embassies. **Undarin**, on the Sarkora where the
Rolling River joined it, the market where the miners of the plateau met the herders of the
steppe; its districts Cliffside, Steppeside and the five holy isles of Gorum's Chain. The demons
found the stockyards useful. **Storasta**, at the meeting of the Sarkora and the West Sellen, a
shaman-ruled city of groves and gardens, independent for centuries and annexed in 4000; the last
city to fall, its sacred circles held by witches and shamans with druids of Mendev and Numeria
beside them, and their last stand is the ***Song of Sarkoris***, which is very beautiful.
**Dyinglight**, in the fens of the far north-west, the spiritual heart of the kingdom; the
priests of Pulura's monastery at **Pulura's Fall** received a vision and the city emptied before
the demons arrived. It is the single instance in this history of a god warning anyone in time.
She is a minor one. **Raliscrad**, the trade town where the Sarkora met the Isk, abandoned in
such haste that the wealth was left on the tables, where the demons found it. **Gundrun**, far to
the south-west, destroyed, then rebuilt around 4670 by people who simply came back, and who have
survived seventy years in the Wound by being beneath notice. **Domora** in the west, where a
god-caller named Hume called a water spirit out of a pool in 4172 to break a Mammoth Lord raid,
and taught the art anew. The pool is still there. **Threshold**, a name at the western lip of
the canyon country, which the old accounts call a prison-tower of the High Cairns where Sarkoris
kept its arcane casters to work under the eyes of the druids. And under the Wolfscrags,
**Jormurdun**, a dwarven Sky Citadel lost since 1600 AR, whose royal line scattered across the
world with pieces of a broken key.

**The diaspora.** The refugees went everywhere, and everywhere had an opinion. Into Mendev, where
they are the Fire Circle and the cave-dwellers of Saresp, and where the Third Crusade's
inquisitors treated their animism as demon-worship and burned them for it. Into Numeria. Into
Ustalav, where the locals call them **leechfolk** and where the northern Shudderwood is haunted
by Sarkorians the Ustalavs murdered, who have at least stayed to make a point. West to the Realm
of the Mammoth Lords, who share their blood and took them in the way one takes in a cousin. A few
as far as Iobaria and Garund, where nobody knows what a Sarkorian is and so nobody minds. And a
stubborn handful never left at all: the folk of Gundrun, the **Tarnshiavs** at Shadow Spring in
the fen, the Kellids of **Sabertooth Vale** (who are not what they were, and would rather you did
not ask), and the humans of **Raliscrad** who chose to work for a demon rather than run. Three
generations on, "Sarkorian" means a great-grandmother's stories, a word or two of Hallit, a
tattoo pattern, a skull in a box, and the certainty that the country over the river is *yours*
and was taken.

What a Fire Circle grandmother keeps, from an inventory a landlord's clerk once tried to make
and gave up on:

> *One (1) skull, elk, wrapped. Not for sale. Not for touching. Not for asking about.
> One (1) knife, the good one. One (1) knife, the other one.
> Four (4) words of Hallit the grandchildren know. Eleven (11) they do not.
> One (1) map of Undarin, drawn from memory, wrong.
> One (1) place set at every meal. Not for Aroden. For someone else. Don't ask about that either.*

**Bio hooks from Sarkoris.** A god-caller's granddaughter with the family god's name and no god,
which puts her ahead of most clerics. A huzuri-keeper whose clan is down to four people, three of
whom are not speaking. A Fire Circle cook who has never seen the country he calls home and cooks
it anyway. A Saresp cave-child. A Kellid raised by crusaders who burned her parents and taught
her to be grateful. A Sarkorian who came home with the crusade's supply wagons because the walls
at Drezen are the first walls on this side of the river a Kellid could sleep behind in
seventy-five years. A crusader built them. A demon kept them. She sleeps fine. Someone from
Gundrun, who has lived his whole life inside the Wound and wants to know what all the fuss is
about.

## 6. The Worldwound: what Sarkoris became

A hundred and seven years ago a mile-long blight rimmed in black flame opened in the barrows of
the Northmounds, and the land came apart around it: a fan of canyons reaching nearly to every
border, rivers gone into their beds and staying there, and a fire that has not gone out since.
The name comes from an old ballad's line about a "wound in the world," the last time a poet was
useful here. The ground itself shifts in front of you the nearer you go to the rift; at the edges
it settles, mostly, and the crusade has built its cities on the mostly. Ashstorms come off it.
The sky over the centre is poison and coloured lightning. Even the animals are tainted. Every
crusader chart of it is a copy of a Sarkorian chart, and most of the names on it are older than
the Wound, because the crusade has never held anything long enough to name it.

The crusade's own recruiters have a sheet for this. It is called *Questions Commonly Asked by
Those Considering Service West of the Sellen*, and it is the most honest document the crusade
has ever printed, because it was written by a clerk who had been there:

> *Is it as bad as they say?* Yes.
> *Is the ground safe to walk on?* The ground is safe to walk on until it is not. You will not be
> warned.
> *What do the demons want?* To watch.
> *Are there people living there?* Yes. Do not assume they want rescuing. Some of them have
> jobs.
> *What is the pay?* The pay is discussed at the citadel.
> *Will I see the four?* No.
> *Will the four see me?* Also no.
> *What should I bring?* Boots. A second pair of boots. Something to be buried with, or a note
> saying you do not mind.

**The five regions**, as the crusade's scholars divide them, from a safe distance:

- **The Wounded Lands**: the east, and the rift itself: what was the Northmounds, now a canyon a
  mile wide and dozens of miles long, full of chaos-stuff and bubbling primordial coagulation.
  **Iz** sits at its heart and **Drezen** on its eastern arm. The crusade's forward citadel and
  the enemy's capital are on the same limb. The **Marchlands**, badlands, canyon and lava west of
  Drezen, are what the crusade calls the part of this it has had to learn; the name is the
  table's, not the charts'.
- **The Stonewilds**: the broken high country to the north, once the vast evergreen **Forest of
  Stones**, birthplace of the Green Faith and keeper of its holiest texts. When it fell, a faction
  of its druids chose undeath rather than defeat, and the **Stonewild Sect** now defends the
  Stonewilds against demon and living alike, having concluded that the living were the problem.
  The **Petrified Library** and **Greengrave Keep** are up there. Nobody in Drezen can say who
  holds either. Nobody in Drezen has volunteered.
- **Riftshadow**: the river country: the Sarkora and its towns, once the most populous part of
  the kingdom, now ruins under demonic barges. **Undarin** at its north end, **Storasta** at its
  south, **Raliscrad** between, and the river running past all three as if nothing had happened.
- **Frostmire**: the frozen north-west: the **Wolfscrags**, whose confusing valleys hold packs of
  dire wolves, and **Frostmire Fen**, huge, frigid marshland broken by sulphurous hot springs.
  Sparsely settled even before, and emptied on Pulura's warning. Now fiendish marsh giants hold
  Dyinglight and have taken up cannibalism, which the demons find a bit much; hezrou dredge the
  fen for sunken relics; and things called bowel worms live in the water. The name is
  descriptive. It is the coldest part of the Wound.
- **The Sarkorian Steppe**: the far west: open, flat, dry, and almost featureless, once grazing,
  crossed by mammoth and woolly rhinoceros and by the raiding war-bands of the **Mammoth Lords**,
  who have always thought of it as theirs and who push the border east a mile at a time and lose
  it again, and have been doing so for a century with the patience of people who have never
  heard of a crusade. The **Shudderwood**, the one great forest left, fills its southern corner,
  and the fey there hate everyone now, evenly.

**The rivers.** The **West Sellen** is the eastern boundary and the road the crusade came in by;
everything east of it is Mendev and everything west of it is this. The **Ahari** ran under
Drezen's walls until the Wound opened; its dry bed now cuts the city in half. The **Sarkora**
still flows, south-west of the rift, out of habit; the **Rolling River** and the **Isk** do not,
and their dry beds are roads now. The **Moutray**, along the Ustalav border, carried wardstones
once. The **Gray Road** west of Drezen is a dry riverbed named for what walks it. The locals
decline to be more specific.

**Who rules.** Nothing, and everything, and it is more stable than Mendev. The Wound is a loose
coalition of demonic warlords, each holding a fief by force or by guile, united only in wanting
Golarion to suffer, which as a political programme has the merit of being achievable. The
greatest is the balor **Khorramzadeh**, the **Storm King**, who rules Iz and took Kenabres. Next
was the marilith **Aponavisius**, who took Drezen in 4638 and held it until Rova; she is not in
it now, and is somewhere. The most respected is the witch **Areelu Vorlesh**, who held Undarin,
who is said to have had a hand in opening the Wound, and whose name is behind the corruption of
the wardstones. Respected, note, not obeyed. Demons do not obey. They are merely impressed. The
lilitu **Minagho** kept Raliscrad, with a staff. Under them, the **Templars of the Ivory
Labyrinth**, Baphomet's secret society, whose cells hide inside respectable families in cities
across the world and who have infiltrated crusader orders before, and the cults of **Deskari**,
Lord of the Locust Host, whose green-and-red locust wings are the sign of the enemy, and whose
entire theology is that everything should be eaten. He has been at this since 4606. He is not in
a hurry. Nobody who is winning is.

**Humans in the Wound.** More than you would think, and fewer than there were. Cultists and
mercenaries who serve the fiends, and who will tell you their employers keep their word; chattel
kept for labour, food or sacrifice, in that order of preference; the willing workers of
Raliscrad; the free folk of Gundrun; the changed people of Sabertooth Vale and Shadow Spring; the
enslaved miners of Iz, who broke every production record under the whip; and the thousands who
lived in occupied Drezen, among whom **Fenna Tusk**, a half-orc girl of seventeen, was born, and
grew up, and is by all accounts cheerful. A character born inside the Wound is possible. Ask
Matt. Ask nicely.

**What you meet on the road.** Babau, brimoraks, vrocks, nabasu, shadow demons; derakni in the
sky; vescavors, colony-swarms that eat metal and skin and are not particular about the order;
plague steeds on the plains between Storasta and Undarin; urannags, midnight morels, the fungus
called Shax's beard, which grows where it likes; ghouls in the chapels; the winter wraiths of the
Crown of the World, raised from Drezen's own dead and still, in some sense, on the garrison
rolls. And the crusade's own losses: seventy-five years of names on the charts, every one of them
somebody's, every one of them marking a place someone was sure was safe.

## 7. Drezen: the city you are standing in

**What it was.** A citadel-city raised in the hubris of the First Crusade, on the eastern arm of
the Wounded Lands, to be the crusade's forward fortress and a monument to the proposition that
the war was as good as won. In 4638 it was betrayed from within by **Staunton Vhane**, a dwarf
paladin turned, and taken by **Aponavisius**; a wave of raw chaos washed over it and turned most
of its people into beast-things that ate each other, and when the slaughter stopped the corpses
were carted to the ice cliffs of the Crown of the World, and from them rose the winter wraiths.
Drezen's dead were put to work before they were cold. **Saint Argil**, the Pauper Sorcerer, died
on the walls covering the flight of those who got out, and was made a saint for it, which is the
crusade's pension scheme. The ***Sword of Valor***, the banner of the crusade, was lost here. The
shrines of Iomedae and Sarenrae were made into mockeries of themselves, by demons who had clearly
studied the originals. For seventy-five years, three generations, it was a demon city of some
seven thousand, and the chronicle calls it the first great wound of the crusade.

**How it was taken.** By a hundred volunteers and four mythic nobodies, in one week of Rova: the
courtyard on the 8th, the sally port and the Templar's Court on the 9th, three days on the first
floor of the citadel, Staunton Vhane dead and buried on the 12th and his glaive unmade in the
forge, and the dungeons on the 13th, where the banner was. **Lupenor** wrecked the enemy's
catapults during the siege, which nobody had thought to do in seventy-five years. **Kamilo
Dann**, who held a dozen wounded in a stone gaol at Vilareth Ford until the ford was retaken, is
one of many small heroes of that fortnight, and yours can be another. Small is a description of
the pay.

**Who runs it now.** In the sense that anyone does.

- **Irabeth Tirabade**, half-orc paladin of the Eagle Watch, Governor. Held Kenabres together
  with her bare hands and was released by the Queen to ride north. On being handed Drezen: "I
  had a city. It fell on me. Give me another."
- **Aron Kir**, former spy, now Warden of Drezen; keeps the citadel's watch and its secrets, and
  has the look of a man who has read your file. He has read your file.
- **Horgus Gwerm**, a Kenabres noble who counted his gold in the rubble and now counts the
  city's grain; blunt, unsentimental, and the man to see about supply. His office door reads:
  *NO. Come in and I will explain why.*
- **Anevia Tirabade**, the Governor's wife and the crusade's agent; her spies map the west.
- **Joran Vhane**, Staunton's kinsman, a dwarven master smith, surrendered in the citadel and
  now a penitent who dreams of relighting the forge beneath Drezen for good ends. Asked whether
  that was not what the last Vhane said, he answered, "He said it better. I'm going to do it."

For new arrivals, the Warden's office issues a single sheet. It is reproduced here in full.

> *ON ARRIVAL IN DREZEN*
> *Where do I sleep?* Where you can. The barracks are full. The inns are full. The temple
> compound has an orphanage, a food bank and an infirmary; qualify for one.
> *Where do I eat?* Rabiah's compound, the kitchen. Do not comment on the angle.
> *Where do I get paid?* The citadel, on completion, on presentation of the work, on the
> Warden's word that the work was done. The Warden's word is not for sale. Do not ask what it is
> for.
> *Is the South Bank safe?* The South Bank is across the river.
> *Is the river safe?* The river is dry.
> *Is the dry river safe?* Use the bridges.
> *Who do I report a demon to?* Anyone. Loudly.
> *Who do I report a crusader to?* The Warden. Quietly.
> *Where is the Queen's steward?* Nerosyan. He was here for an afternoon. We have his chair.

**Where things are.** The crusade's chart of the city is the Map tab on this site; the letters
here are its letters. North is to the upper right, and the citadel walls are a little under a
mile from the South Bank.

- **Citadel Drezen** (A): the star-fort in its moat on the high ground, inside a pentagonal
  curtain wall with six towers and one gate facing south into the city: the sally port, the
  Templar's Court, the tiers and dungeons the four cleared. The crusade holds it outright, and
  the garrison lives here, behind the walls. Everything the company is paid to do is counted out
  of this arch and counted back through it. The counting is exact both ways.
- **Iomedae's Preservers** (B): **Harlock**'s order, walled on the high ground north-east of the
  city: garrison, barracks, chapel, stable, a watchtower over the western approach, and a parade
  ground drilled every morning. Five squads of paladins and clerics sworn to hold the city.
  **Elara Dawnstrider**, a paladin who renounced her noble family and has not said what they did,
  holds the order while Harlock is west and calls its recruits. On the trials: "Three days. The
  first day tells me if you can stand. The second tells me if you can stand it. The third is for
  the ones I'm going to keep, so they can eat." **Aldwin Brightblade**, once a Kenabres
  blacksmith, is one of them, and misses the forge.
- **Varic's temple compound** (C): on the eastern ridge, ringed by its own low wall. At the
  centre, the **temple of Sarenrae**, **Varic**'s foundation; its head priest **Rothin Vald** is
  two days west keeping the reopened Hidden Temple, leaving the temple in Drezen with a founder
  in the west, a head priest in the west, and a congregation. Across the way, the **temple of
  the united faiths**, **Sosiel Vaenic**'s: a priest of Shelyn, charged with building one house
  for all the good gods of the crusade. Asked how the gods were getting along under one roof, he
  said, "Better than their priests. I've had to move the pews twice." An orphanage, a food bank
  and an infirmary fill the rest of the ground.
- **Rabiah's compound** (D): the ring quarter east of the ridge, wound in circles around the
  old **amphitheatre**, pre-crusade stone and the one place in the city that will hold a crowd:
  bazaar, brewery, bathhouse, community kitchen, workshop row, and a shrine over a meeting
  square. Built fast and built crooked. The kitchen and the dining hall were begun from two ends
  by two people and met at the wrong angle, and the L-shaped result is known throughout Drezen
  as **Tuesday's mistake**. Wednesday, they started over there. The four's own followers live
  here, an old guard from Kenabres and a newer wave since. **Tilda Merrow**, an aging adept,
  keeps them from dying of their own enthusiasm; she describes her duties as "splints, soup, and
  saying no." **Sir Brannic Vayne**, who is not really a knight but has beautiful handwriting,
  keeps the lists. The handwriting is how he got the title. He has never denied it. He has never
  confirmed it. He has a seal.
- **The Celeste Market District** (E): **Lupenor**'s foundation, packed between the citadel
  wall and the riverbank: vendors under canvas, an auction house, warehouses along the wall,
  three inns, and Lupenor's residence above it all. **Mira Thistledance**, a half-elf bard,
  screens every trader who wants a stall. Asked her criteria, she said, "Yes." **Silas Thorne**
  sells cloth, in a city where everyone's clothes are ruined, and is doing very well. **Abner
  Suthi** (never "Avner") trades honey and sweets, and once died on a spike above a lava lake
  and was brought back. He sells sweets. He has thought about it. The Market hires people with
  no company behind them for work it does not want traced to itself, and does not apologise for
  it. From the board outside the auction house, this week:
  > *ROPE, new, Kenabres: 4 sp the fathom.*
  > *ROPE, used, South Bank: ask.*
  > *HONEY, Suthi's: 2 sp the jar. Sweets: 1 cp, or a story he hasn't heard.*
  > *CLOTH, Thorne's: whatever you have. He will make it work.*
  > *WANTED: persons of no company for a job of no description. Office above. Knock once.*
- **The shrine of Erastil** in the southern quarter: reopened by **Jeskar Hinton**, a young
  priest who came with the first refugees, and found desecrated once already. For a shrine in
  Drezen that is a good first month.
- **The Ahari riverbed**: dry since the Wound opened, cutting the city in half, crossed by two
  bridges, and a road for anything that would rather not use the gates.
- **The South Bank** (F): the quarter across the riverbed: scrap yards and rope-walks that spent
  three generations under demon rule and have not been searched. **Cinder Row** runs through it.
  The crusade holds the walls above it and very little of what is under its roofs. This is where
  the work is.
- **The cemetery**: outside the walls to the west, on the far bank. Older than the occupation,
  and added to steadily since the retaking. The only district in Drezen with a growing
  population.
- **The crusaders' lines and the refugees**: neat rows of identical tents beside the merchants'
  stalls, and the people out of Kenabres and the lost settlements, still arriving, still being
  told the city is not ready for them, still staying.

**The roads.** East and south, the road down to **Kenabres**: **Keeper's Canyon**, where the road
turns toward Drezen and where two armies camped, one of which is still there in pieces;
**Vilareth Ford**, the crossing on the road up from Kenabres; about a week's march for a column,
less for a rider. West, the road to the **Hidden Temple**: two days on foot, with nothing on it.
"Nothing" is a technical term. Beyond that, the **Gray Road**, the **Weeping Hills**, the lava
country, and everything the Marchlands map on the crusade's site shows, which the company will
not be asked to visit, until it is. North, above the city, the **Lake Lost to the Sun**, named
for the light that does not reach it. A hex on the Marchlands chart is twelve miles, or a hard
day's march.

**What Drezen feels like.** A garrison learning to be a town, and the town learning what a
garrison thinks a town is for. Acid-scarred stone. Two temples facing each other across a
street, both newer than the season. More buildings than people, and nobody sure what is in most
of them, and a standing policy of not finding out. Every party that goes west leaves by the
western gate and is counted home through the same arch. The ledger at the gate has three
columns: *Out*, *In*, and *Shrinkage*. It is the most complete record Drezen keeps.

## 8. Building the bio: prompts

Answer the ones that fit. Three or four good answers make a character. Nobody in Drezen has all
of them about themselves either.

**Where were you on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus, 4713?** Everyone in Mendev knows where they
were. It is this generation's "the day the wardstone cracked." The previous generation's was
also the day the wardstone cracked. This is a country with one story and a good memory.

**Which Mendev are you from?** Native, crusader's-child, Sarkorian, or none of the above (a
tiefling of the Wound; a mongrelman; a foreigner off the last boat, not yet disillusioned). What
does your family think of the other two? What did your family *do* to the other two?

**How did you get to Drezen?** Some of the ways the table already has.

1. **With the hundred.** You marched out of Kenabres on the 30th of Arodus with the four. You
   were at Vilareth Ford or Keeper's Canyon. You are one of the volunteers who lived. There is no
   bed for you.
2. **With the relief.** You came up with the reinforcement and supply that has followed since
   Rova. You arrived after the fighting and before the pay.
3. **On a supply wagon.** Conscripted in Nerosyan, or hired on in Egede, or a stowaway. Twelve
   left. Nine arrived. You are one of the nine, or you are one of the three and nobody has
   noticed you are here.
4. **With the refugees.** From Kenabres, from a farm the demons burned, from Valas's Gift. You
   fled a demon-haunted city toward the demons, because that was the direction the road went.
5. **A deserter.** From a crusader company, a mercenary band, or the inquisition. Drezen is the
   one place nobody checks papers. Nobody in Drezen has any.
6. **A pilgrim.** You came for the banner, or the ballad, or the four. The banner is in a citadel
   you are not allowed into. The four are in the west. The ballad is everywhere, and the verse
   about the forty at the door is now the sixty at the door.
7. **A low templar.** You came for the pay. See the recruiters' sheet: the pay is discussed at
   the citadel.
8. **Home.** You are Sarkorian, and these are the first walls on this side of the river a Kellid
   could sleep behind in three generations. A crusader built them. A demon kept them. You sleep
   fine.
9. **You never left.** You were born in occupied Drezen. (Talk to Matt. Expect questions.)
10. **None of these.** You have a reason of your own, and it is a good one, and you have told it
    to no one, and the Warden has it on file already.

**Who do you already know?** Any of the Drezen citizens in §7, at one line's worth: you sold
Horgus Gwerm a wagon, and he still thinks he was robbed; you took Elara Dawnstrider's three-day
trial and lasted two, and gave back the boots; Sosiel Vaenic buried your brother and did it
beautifully; Tilda Merrow set your arm, and it was the other arm; you ate at Tuesday's mistake
and sat in the corner where the walls meet, which is where the draught is. The crusade's
chronicle has the full entries for each. The one-line version is all you need to claim, and all
they will admit to.

**What do you believe?** Iomedae, and how much, and since when; the Dawnflower; Shelyn; Desna;
Erastil; Abadar, who at least has a contract; Pharasma, who will see you regardless; Gorum;
Cayden Cailean, whose theology is available by the pint; the Green Faith; the clan's god, whom
you have never met; Aroden, for whom a place is still set; nothing, since Armasse, which is the
fastest-growing faith in Mendev. What is the gesture you make when you pass the temples? Do it
now. Think about what it does.

**What do you carry?** A zoic fetish that has protected you from everything except what
happened. A skull in a box, which is a relative. A banner you did not earn. A banner you did,
which is worse, because you know the price. A sword-and-sun badge on a coat that is not yours,
from a man who no longer needs the coat. A relic of the pirate days, for when the crusade is
over. A brewery token, the one currency that has held its value. A bowl from Tuesday's mistake,
which you were supposed to return. Twelve years' service and a scar you explain differently every
time.

**What did the Third Crusade do to your family?** If you are native Mendevian or Sarkorian, the
answer is probably not "nothing." If you are crusader stock, the answer is probably in the other
column.

**What does the word "wardstone" mean to you now?** It meant a wall. Then it meant a wall that
could crack. Then it meant a wall that four strangers broke on purpose, and everyone cheered.
Work forward from there.

**What do you want from Drezen?** Pay. A wall. A name. A grave to visit. A god to find. A way
west. A way to never go west again. A quiet drink in a city with one brewery and no quiet. To be
in the *In* column. Everyone in Drezen wants one of these and is going to get a different one.

### Naming by people

- **Native Mendevian**: Iobarian roots; sturdy, slightly Slavic-sounding, the names of people
  who expected to be here a while. *Hulrun Shappok, Irabeth Tirabade, Anevia, Sosiel Vaenic,
  Nestrin Alodae, Eterrius Sunnestier, Kamilo Dann, Trudean Delashaw, Jeskar Hinton, Tilda
  Merrow.*
- **Crusader stock**: southern names from anywhere: Taldan (*Galfrey, Aron Kir, Aldrich Thorne,
  Brannic Vayne, Dyre, Marcovina*), Chelaxian, Andoren, Varisian. A family name that is a place
  ("of Lauterbury") is a knightly affectation, and usually a place that has never heard of them.
- **Sarkorian / Kellid**: short and hard: *Dron, Gurog, Kronug, Belka, Inkit, Yala*; the clan
  name over the given name; a longer, stranger name for the notable: *Uloric Dziergas, Domora
  Hume, Khraigorr Half-Face, Tarnshiav, Widowknife, Farheaven.* Nobody in Sarkoris was ever
  called something they could not shout across a valley.
- **Mongrelmen of Neathholm**: single, plain, given underground by people who did not expect
  anyone above to ask: *Lann, Chusul, Opoli, Chyrrik.*
- **Drezen-born**: whatever the demons let them be called. *Fenna Tusk* is the one example, and
  she seems to have chosen it herself.
