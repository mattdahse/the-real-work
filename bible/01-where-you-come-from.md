# Where You Come From — A Character Bio Helper

*The Real Work — the lands around Drezen, for anyone writing a character who is standing in the
city in the autumn of 4713 AR*

This is a handout for building a backstory, not a chapter and not a rulebook. It gathers what is
true about the country around **Drezen** — the Worldwound, old Sarkoris, Mendev and its capital
Nerosyan — from Paizo's published setting and from the crusade's own chronicle at
[The Fifth Crusade](https://mattdahse.github.io/the-fifth-crusade/), and it stops at the present
day. Nothing here is a spoiler for what the mythic four are doing in the west, and nothing here
reaches past the moment the company is living in.

**Which source wins.** Where Paizo and the chronicle differ, the chronicle governs at this table:
its spellings, its dates, its people. Where the chronicle is silent, Paizo's setting stands. Where
both are silent, invent, and tell Matt so it can be written down.

**How to use it.** Skim §1 so you know what everyone in the city knows. Then read the one or two
sections that match where your character is from, pick from the prompts in §8, and check §9 before
you claim anything that belongs to the crusade's story already.

## 1. The moment you are standing in

It is **Neth, 4713 AR** — late autumn, with the first hard frosts on the Marchlands and the
southern horizon lit orange after dark. Everyone in Drezen, whoever they are, carries these
facts:

- **Kenabres fell on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus** — Iomedae's own feast day, some three months ago.
  The Storm King came over the walls, the silver dragon **Terendelev** died in the sky above the
  city, the great bridge went down, and the wardstone in the Kite was shattered. The city that
  had been the crusade's northern wall for a century fell in an afternoon. Prelate **Hulrun**
  was among the first dead.
- **The wardstones are gone.** A week later, at the **Gray Garrison**, four nobodies out of the
  rubble broke the corrupted fragment before the enemy could turn it against the army gathering
  at the border, and every wardstone along the West Sellen went dark with it. The border that
  held for seventy-five years is now a river and nothing more. What the stones held has not gone
  out of the world; it went somewhere, and the songs say where.
- **The four** are **Rabiah**, a red-haired sorcerer of sixteen; **Harlock Greyforge**, a
  half-orc of Iomedae who carries a sword that talks; **Lupenor Celest**, an elven archer of
  Desna; and **Varic Sarian**, a half-elf priest of the Dawnflower. The table calls them
  **Rabiah's Redeemers**. Mendev calls them the Wardstone's Champions, and there is a ballad,
  and it is sung in every tavern between here and Egede whether the four like it or not.
- **Queen Galfrey rode north** to Kenabres herself, and sent the four to Drezen with a hundred
  volunteers who privately believed they were riding to their deaths. They marched out on the
  30th of Arodus, took **Vilareth Ford** back on the road, refused to walk into **Keeper's
  Canyon** in the dark, and came within sight of Drezen's walls on the 6th of Rova.
- **Drezen changed hands on Fire Day, the 13th of Rova, 4713.** The citadel that fell to the
  demons in 4638 is a crusader city again. The ***Sword of Valor***, the banner lost when it
  fell, was carried out of the dungeons beneath it. **Staunton Vhane**, the dwarf who betrayed
  the city three generations ago, is buried here.
- **Sir Aldrich Thorne** came from the Queen as steward the next day, and the city passed to
  **Irabeth Tirabade** to govern. The crusade holds the walls and the citadel. It does not yet
  hold every quarter.
- **The four are away in the west**, days out of contact, and what they are doing there is not
  common knowledge. The cohorts they left behind are holding the city on a thread, and hiring.

If your character has been in Drezen more than a week, they have also heard: that there is a
thousand-platinum bounty posted by the demons themselves on a succubus somewhere in the west;
that a shrine of Sarenrae two days down the western road has been reopened and is being kept by
a priest named **Rothin Vald**; that the **Southshore** has not been searched; and that
**Horgus Gwerm**, the quartermaster, is browbeating the crown for grain.

## 2. Mendev — the crusader state

**The land.** Mendev is the northern end of the continent: snow-covered mountains, icy steppe,
and the great evergreen **Estrovian Forest** across its centre and south. North, the ice cliffs
of the Crown of the World. East, across the forest, the **Lake of Mists and Veils**, which every
crusader crossed to get here. South, across the rivers, **Numeria** — a harsh country of savages
and alien metal that Mendevians want nothing to do with. West, the **West Sellen River**, and on
its far bank the Worldwound. There is almost no arable land. The country imports nearly
everything, and for a century the crusade has been fed by boats: crusaders and grain coming up
the Sellen and across the lake, arriving weekly at **Egede**.

**Three kinds of Mendevian.** Nobody in Mendev is just "a Mendevian." Ask which:

- **The natives.** Descended from Iobarian exiles, semi-nomadic once, and culturally cousins to
  the Sarkorians across the river. Many still keep the old **animist** faith or the **Green
  Faith**, and quietly. The crusaders have treated them as second-class for a hundred years, and
  in the Third Crusade burned them for heresy. A native Mendevian character has a grandmother who
  remembers who informed on whom.
- **The crusaders and their children.** From Cheliax, Isger, Andoran, Taldor and everywhere
  else. The first wave were the finest people of the south: clerics, paladins, knights who sold
  their lands to come. By the Fourth Crusade the boats were bringing mercenaries, dispossessed
  nobles, wandering companies, political undesirables and men let out of prison on condition
  they went north, and by now they outnumber the pious. Southern governments found the crusade a
  convenient place to send people they did not want. A crusader's-child character can be
  third-generation Mendevian and still be called a foreigner by the natives.
- **The Sarkorians.** Kellid refugees of the lost kingdom, three generations in exile. See §5.
  In Nerosyan they have a quarter of their own along the western wall, the **Fire Circle**, with
  shops and lodgings kept in the old style. Others took the caves of **Saresp Mountain** in the
  far east, or scattered into Numeria, Ustalav and beyond.

And among them: **tieflings** in unusual numbers (Mendev is home to many asura-spawn, and the
Wound breeds pitborn); **dwarves** whose ancestors fled the lost Sky Citadel of **Jormurdun** and
who have mostly come down in the world, many living poor in the Fleshward slum of Chesed on the
lake; halflings; half-elves; and beneath Kenabres, the **mongrelmen**, the warped descendants of
First Crusade soldiers who were driven underground by their own comrades.

**Rule.** Mendev is a monarchy under **Queen Galfrey**, the Sword of Iomedae — a Taldan woman who
looks not yet thirty and is over a hundred years old, kept young by the sun orchid elixir and
crowned in 4601 as a paladin of Aroden, before there was a Worldwound. She retrained herself as
Iomedae's paladin when the crusades came, and she is the one who ordered the retreat from
Sarkoris in 4638 so that the wardstones could be raised. Her word is law within Nerosyan's walls.
Beyond them, every commander of every city, fort and army interprets her commands a little
differently, and some take more liberty than others. Mendev has lived under martial law so long
that no one remembers anything else. Until this summer, the second most powerful person in the
kingdom was **Prelate Hulrun Shappok** of Kenabres, the old inquisitor whose witch-burnings as a
young man started the Third Crusade, and who only in his last years began to understand the shame
he had brought on the faith. He is dead now.

**Faith.** **Iomedae**, the Inheritor, is the crusade. Her white-and-gold banner is the one all
crusaders nominally fight under, her cathedral is the centre of Nerosyan, and her holy day
**Armasse** is the day Kenabres fell. Beside her: the remnants of Aroden's church, who began the
First Crusade; **Sarenrae**, whose priests march with the columns; **Shelyn**, **Desna**,
**Erastil**, **Abadar**, **Pharasma**. **Gorum** has grown popular in the barracks of Egede for
obvious reasons, and **Cayden Cailean**'s faithful have a whole neighbourhood in Nerosyan. The
Green Faith and the animist ways of the natives survive, and are looked at sideways. Every
Mendevian, pious or not, knows the gesture: fingers to the lips, then pressed to the sword-pommel,
as one crosses oneself before something sacred.

**Orders and outfits.** A crusader belongs to something, or pretends to:

- **The Eagle Watch** — the crusaders of Kenabres. **Irabeth Tirabade** commanded them at the
  Defender's Heart; **Anevia**, her wife, served among them; Harlock was on the eve of his oath to
  them when the city fell.
- **The Order of Heralds** — the knightly order that curbed the Third Crusade's purges and turned
  the war back outward. Still the conscience of the crusade, such as it has.
- **The Riftwardens** — a secretive society of planar scholars who want the Worldwound *closed*,
  not merely held, based in Kenabres's **Librarium of the Broken Black Wing**. **Aravashnial**,
  the blind elven wizard, is one. Their mark is the Seeker's Spiral. *See §9 before you touch
  this.*
- **The Kenabres Wolves** — the spies and scouts, **Aron Kir**'s people. He is Warden of Drezen
  now.
- **The Order of Holy Sentinels** — guardians of the ***Demonscope*** at **Clydwell Keep**, the
  greatest of the border forts. Every fort on the old wardstone line is named for a dead general.
- **The knightly orders** — the Emerald Sword, the Flaming Lance, the Rampant Dragon, the
  Sunrise Sword, the Guild of Diggers and Sappers, and dozens more. Some are ancient. Some were
  invented on the boat, because the heralds of Iomedae cannot check every newcomer's claim to a
  title, and everyone knows it.
- **The Low Templars** — the crusade's own name for its mercenaries, sellswords and adventurers
  who fight for pay or plunder rather than faith. Not an insult, exactly. Not a compliment.
- **The Pathfinder Society** — present in Nerosyan since the Queen gave them the Starrise Spire in
  4707, and sending expeditions into the Wound disguised as mercenary companies.
- **The inquisitors** — Hulrun's people. Fewer than they were, and quieter since Kenabres, but
  the cleansing fire still glows strongest there.

**Banners.** Any crusader stronghold is a riot of colour: streamers and flags from hundreds of
clans, counties, kingdoms and families competing for room. The banners that matter are the ones
earned on the field, and they take the form of Iomedae's own symbols — the **sword**, the
**shield**, or the **sunburst** — and are granted to soldiers of any rank.

**Speech.** Mendevians speak **Taldane** (Common); the natives and the Sarkorians also speak
**Hallit**, the Kellid tongue, which has no written form. Two words of Mendevian slang worth
knowing: a **gaffle** or a **rip** is a scam.

**The wardstones, and their absence.** For seventy-five years the border was a line of
rune-carved menhirs along the West Sellen and the Moutray, infused with **nexovar** from the hills
of the Egelsee, maintained by daily prayer and a yearly ritual, and housed in a chain of forts.
Nothing chaotic or evil could pass between them, and no teleportation could cross the line. They
are gone since Arodus. A character who served on the wardstone line — a priest who kept the
prayers, a soldier in one of the named forts — has lost the whole meaning of their posting in a
single week.

## 3. Nerosyan — the Diamond of the North

The capital, and the crusade's fortress. Before the Worldwound it was a run-down river town of
pirates; the First Crusade widened its streets, walled it, and filled it with soldiers, and the
piracy never entirely left. Population about sixty-five thousand, nearly all human.

**Where it sits.** At the junction where the **Egelsee River** runs into the northernmost branch
of the **West Sellen**. The Egelsee is the city's only clean water; the Sellen comes down too
close to the Wound to drink. Everything outside the walls has been burned flat by demonic raids,
on purpose, so that nothing approaches unseen.

**How it is built.** A diamond between the two rivers, so that every wall has water in front of
it. The outer walls are twenty feet thick, crenellated and towered, and the wall-tops are
*roofed* — sloped roofs cut with arrow slits, because the enemy flies. At the centre stands the
**Cruciform Cathedral** of Iomedae, and the avenues run wide and straight from it so that a
sortie can reach any wall in minutes. Four great towers hold the points: the **Battle Tower** in
the north, the most heavily fortified structure in Mendev; the **Woundward Tower**, which holds
the city's wardstone and its best archers and engines against attack from the air; the
**Confluence Tower**, guarding the rivers and sheltering Crusader's Quay; and the **Egelsee
Tower**, the cavalry's staging ground. The **Sellen Docks** are stone quays cut into the bedrock.
Above it all burns the **Starrise Spire**, older than the walls, with a ball of light floating
over it that has not gone out in centuries — a beacon to the crusade and a warning to the Wound.

**The districts.** Battle, Egelsee, Confluence and Woundward are the four quarters. Within them:
**Pilgrim's Rest**, where the endless stream of Iomedaean pilgrims lodges; **Bannerbright**,
where the knightly and clerical orders keep their houses; the **Two Cs**, the neighbourhood of
Cayden Cailean's faithful, which is where the taverns are; the **Fire Circle** along the western
wall, where the Sarkorian refugees keep their shops and eating houses in the old style. On the
east side, **Hank the Alerunner's** hideout is a tavern full of relics of the city's pirate
days. **Ancuin's Brewery** has been the crusaders' favourite for a hundred years.

**Who runs it.** The Queen, from her palace, counselled by an angel named **Opaline**. Around her:
**Lord Inquisitor Casori**; **Captain Jaspar** of the Crusader Heralds; Venture-Captain **Jorsal
of Lauterbury** of the Pathfinders. The city is law-abiding, beautiful in its design, and entirely
clear about what it is for.

**What has happened lately.** For years the city has been harried by things that come up the
rivers — merrow, scrags, grindylows, the water-things that serve the hezrou that rules ruined
Storasta downriver — and there are stories of a larger siege this year, turned back with the
Pathfinders in the thick of it. The wagons of conscripts and supply have been rolling north to
Kenabres and on to Drezen since Rova.

**Bio hooks from Nerosyan.** A dock-hand on the Sellen quays. A cathedral acolyte who has never
been outside the walls. A Two Cs tavern brawler. A child of the Fire Circle who speaks Hallit at
home and Taldane in the street. A brewery drayman. A conscript caught stealing bread and put on a
supply wagon, who slipped away the moment it passed Drezen's gate (that is how **Tam**, the
Redeemers' water-carrier, arrived, and Tam is not the only one). A clerk in the palace who saw
the Queen's letter from Drezen before it was read aloud.

## 4. Kenabres and the border — what fell, and what stands

**Kenabres** was the crusade's northern city: twelve thousand people on graduated tiers rising
from the eastern plain, its western edge a cliff above the West Sellen, its water drawn from the
river below. **Old Kenabres** at the top held the oldest families and the administration; the
**Ring District** around it was the rest of the original town; **New Kenabres** was built after
the Worldwound opened, for refugees of the lost settlements, and the **Gate District** ten years
later when New Kenabres overflowed. The **Truestone Quarry**, ten miles east, sent stone weekly to
the Southgate. The road to Egede is 225 miles and ten days by caravan, skirting the Estrovian
Forest past the **Chapel of the Argent Shield**, a fortified temple that was Aroden's and is now
Iomedae's.

It was also the crusade's conscience and its shame: the seat of Hulrun's inquisition, where the
Third Crusade's fires burned hottest, and where the generals **Dyre** and **Marcovina** fought the
Prelate for every soldier he wanted kept home to hunt heretics. The **Kite** held the first
wardstone ever raised. The **Cathedral of Saint Clydwell** stood in the old town. The **Blackwing
Library** held the crusade's greatest store of learning on the demons and the planes.

**What is left.** Half the population — about six and a half thousand — in a city split by
rifts, its cathedral and the Kite destroyed, the library partly collapsed, and the Queen's own
appointee in the Prelate's chair. The **Defender's Heart**, the fortified inn where Irabeth held
the survivors together, is the heart of what remains. The assault tore open layers of tunnels
under the city, and out of them came the mongrelmen of **Neathholm** — a hamlet of a few dozen on
an island in an underground lake, under its one-eyed chief **Chusul**, descended from First
Crusade soldiers whom the Wound changed and whom the crusade then tried to kill. **Chyrrik**, who marched with the four,
is one of them, and there are mongrelmen in the common room of the Defender's Heart now who did
not exist in anyone's world three months ago.

**The road north.** **Valas's Gift**, a village of a couple of hundred on the road north of
Kenabres, named for a holy man and its red-water springs. The **Vineyard Hills Waystation**, a
day's ride south-east of the city, a crossroads outpost well behind the old wardstone line — the
last ordinary place the four stood before the march. The **Estrovian Forest** itself, where few
go willingly: hernes, stag-horned things that were once trespassing huntsmen; witchwargs; a pack
of owlbears that may be ghosts; taiga giants driven in by the opening of the Wound; and the
werebear-kin, respected as the only guides who will take you through. **Clydwell Keep** and the
named forts of the wardstone line, now guarding a line that is not there. And far off, the cursed
ruins of **Icerift Castle**, about which people do not talk.

**Egede**, on the lake. The second city, nearly forty thousand, the end of the River Road and the
first Mendevian ground most crusaders ever set foot on. Where exhausted soldiers are sent to
rest, which means prayer at the temples or the covert brothels, and where merchants have made an
industry of bankrupting pilgrims on their way to the war. Aroden drowned a demon lord here, and
Egedens celebrate **Drowning Day** for twenty-four hours in a way outsiders find disturbing.

**Bio hooks from Kenabres.** Where were you on Armasse? In the Gate District when the bridge
came down. In the quarry caravan, ten miles out, watching the sky. In the cells of the
inquisition. In the tunnels, and you did not know the tunnels were there. Among the refugees
sheltering in the Defender's Heart when four filthy strangers walked back in with the wardstone
broken behind them. A city watchman with twelve years' service and a burn scar you explain
differently every time (that is **Sera Vost**, and she went west; there are others like her). A
craftsman's son who followed Harlock (that is **Deren Ashfall**; there are others like him). A
carpenter who lost workshop, tools and wife in an afternoon (that is **Tobias Runt**). A
mongrelman of Neathholm above ground for the first time.

## 5. Old Sarkoris — the country that was

For a thousand years before the Worldwound, the land west of the Sellen was **Sarkoris**: a
savage, proud, painted Kellid kingdom of fiercely independent clans, founded in 3672 when a
confederation of tribes broke the frost giants of the Northmounds. Everything on the crusade's
charts west of the river is Sarkorian — the steadings, the shrines, the holdfasts — because the
crusade has never had anything better to put in their place.

**The people.** **Kellids**: lean, dark-haired, light-brown-skinned, with dark eyes and scars they
are proud of, because a Kellid without scars is thought to have lived too comfortably. They dress
in leather, fur and hide, and wear **zoic fetishes**, little charms of twine, hair and stone that
their druids enchant. They value strength, bravery and cunning; they have little patience for
compromise, carelessness or weak leaders; they carry only what the clan needs; they respect Ulfen
and dwarves as kindred spirits and distrust magic and machines both, having lived between Irrisen's
witches and Numeria's engines. The **clan** comes before the self. Names are short and
percussive, two syllables or fewer — **Dron**, **Gurog**, **Kronug**; **Belka**, **Inkit**,
**Yala** — and second names are rare, kept for the distinguished or the well-born; a clan name
matters more than either. Sarkorian names of record run longer and stranger: **Uloric Dziergas**
the warlord, **Domora Hume** the god-caller, **Khraigorr Half-Face**, the **Tarnshiavs** of
Dyinglight, the **Widowknife** clanhold, the **Farheaven** clan.

**The clans and their rulers.** Sarkoris was never one thing. Each clan-hold had its
**clanliege**; the great cities had their **witch-wardens**; and warlords rose and fell. The
mystics and witches of Sarkoris were a byword across Avistan, and it was their oration that
warned, in the years before 4606, of ascendant chaos and a thinning between the worlds. No one
listened, because Aroden was prophesied to return and settle everything.

**God-calling.** The heart of Sarkorian religion. A **god-caller** binds a spirit — the clan's
own patron, usually in the shape of a native beast made strange, a bear with feathers, a wolf with
wings — and calls it into the world, where the clan worships it as their god. The forms were
consistent across generations; marriages between clans produced hybrid gods. Southern churches
call this the worship of false gods. The Sarkorians called it the only religion that ever
answered when spoken to. Many of the clan-gods were destroyed or driven off in the first years of
the Wound, and a Sarkorian character of god-calling blood may carry a name for a god no one has
seen in a hundred years.

**The old gods.** Besides the clan-spirits: **Pulura**, the Shimmering Maiden, Mistress of the
Stars and the northern lights, honoured at **Dyinglight** with a great ring of idols, and paired
with **Kostchtchie** the demon of cold as the two faces of winter; the **Green Faith**, born in the
Forest of Stones; **Gorum**, whom Kellids insist was one of their own; **Desna**, whom they see not
as an elf but as a Kellid woman on an elk who leads them to game; **Erastil**; **Pharasma** at
Raliscrad; and names now half-lost — **Alglenweis**, **Dolok Darkfur**, **Kagia**,
**Ristrentho**, the **Stag Mother** of the Forest of Stones, **Sturovenen** the Dragoneagle and
his dawn-feathered children. There was worship of Sarenrae here too, in the old days, in shrines
like the one two days west of Drezen; **Brix Copperfinch**, a gnome scholar with the expedition,
has made a life's study of it. And there was always Deskari's cult in the Northmounds, driven out
by Aroden and never quite gone.

**Huzuris.** The clans preserved the memories of their chiefs and witch-wardens in the skulls of
animals bound to the clan's story — the **huzuri**, in which the clan's soul was held. A clan that
lost its huzuri had lost itself. Some came out with the refugees. Most did not.

**The cities.** **Iz**, the capital, a centre of stone and metalwork beside its mines, and the
first to fall; two-thirds of it went into the rift the day it opened. **Undarin**, on the Sarkora
where the Rolling River joined it, the market where the miners of the plateau met the herders of
the steppe — its districts Cliffside, Steppeside and the five holy isles of Gorum's Chain.
**Storasta**, at the meeting of the Sarkora and the West Sellen, a shaman-ruled city of groves
and gardens, independent for centuries and annexed in 4000; the last city to fall, its sacred
circles held by witches and shamans with druids of Mendev and Numeria beside them, and their last
stand is the ***Song of Sarkoris***. **Dyinglight**, in the fens of the far north-west, the
spiritual heart of the kingdom; the priests of Pulura's monastery at **Pulura's Fall** received a
vision and the city emptied before the demons arrived, its people scattering west to the Mammoth
Lords and south into Ustalav, Numeria and Mendev. **Raliscrad**, the trade town where the Sarkora
met the Isk, abandoned in such haste that the wealth was left on the tables. **Gundrun**, far to
the south-west, destroyed, then rebuilt around 4670 by people who simply came back. **Domora** in
the west, where a god-caller named Hume called a water spirit out of a pool in 4172 to break a
Mammoth Lord raid, and taught the art anew. **Threshold**, a name at the western lip of the
canyon country, which the old accounts call a prison-tower of the High Cairns where Sarkoris kept
its arcane casters to work under the eyes of the druids. And under the Wolfscrags, **Jormurdun**, a dwarven Sky Citadel lost since 1600 AR,
whose royal line scattered across the world with pieces of a broken key.

**The diaspora.** The refugees went everywhere. Into Mendev, where they are the Fire Circle and
the cave-dwellers of Saresp, and where the Third Crusade's inquisitors treated their animism as
demon-worship and burned them for it. Into Numeria. Into Ustalav, where the locals call them
**leechfolk** and where the northern Shudderwood is haunted by Sarkorians the Ustalavs murdered.
West to the Realm of the Mammoth Lords, who share their blood. A few as far as Iobaria and
Garund. And a stubborn handful never left at all: the folk of Gundrun, the **Tarnshiavs** at
Shadow Spring in the fen, the Kellids of **Sabertooth Vale** (who are not what they were), and
the humans of **Raliscrad** who chose to work for a demon rather than run. Three generations on,
"Sarkorian" means a great-grandmother's stories, a word or two of Hallit, a tattoo pattern, a
skull in a box, and the certainty that the country over the river is *yours* and was taken.

**Bio hooks from Sarkoris.** A god-caller's granddaughter with the family god's name and no god.
A huzuri-keeper whose clan is down to four people. A Fire Circle cook who has never seen the
country he calls home. A Saresp cave-child. A Kellid raised by crusaders who burned her parents. A
Sarkorian who came home with the crusade's supply wagons because the walls at Drezen are the first
Sarkorian walls a Kellid could sleep behind in seventy-five years. Someone from Gundrun, who has
lived his whole life inside the Wound and wants to know what all the fuss is about.

## 6. The Worldwound — what Sarkoris became

A hundred and seven years ago a mile-long blight rimmed in black flame opened in the barrows of
the Northmounds, and the land came apart around it: a fan of canyons reaching nearly to every
border, rivers gone into their beds and staying there, and a fire that has not gone out since.
The name comes from an old ballad's line about a "wound in the world." The ground itself shifts
in front of you the nearer you go to the rift; at the edges it settles. Ashstorms come off it.
The sky over the centre is poison and coloured lightning. Even the animals are tainted. Every
crusader chart of it is a copy of a Sarkorian chart, and most of the names on it are older than
the Wound.

**The five regions**, as the crusade's scholars divide them:

- **The Wounded Lands** — the east, and the rift itself: what was the Northmounds, now a canyon
  a mile wide and dozens of miles long, full of chaos-stuff and bubbling primordial coagulation.
  **Iz** sits at its heart and **Drezen** on its eastern arm. The **Marchlands** — badlands,
  canyon and lava west of Drezen — are what the crusade calls the part of this it has had to
  learn; the name is the table's, not the charts'.
- **The Stonewilds** — the broken high country to the north, once the vast evergreen **Forest of
  Stones**, birthplace of the Green Faith and keeper of its holiest texts. When it fell, a faction
  of its druids chose undeath rather than defeat, and the **Stonewild Sect** now defends the
  Stonewilds against demon and living alike. The **Petrified Library** and **Greengrave Keep** are
  up there, and nobody in Drezen can say who holds either.
- **Riftshadow** — the river country: the Sarkora and its towns, once the most populous part of
  the kingdom, now ruins under demonic barges. **Undarin** at its north end, **Storasta** at its
  south, **Raliscrad** between.
- **Frostmire** — the frozen north-west: the **Wolfscrags**, whose confusing valleys hold packs of
  dire wolves, and **Frostmire Fen**, huge, frigid marshland broken by sulphurous hot springs.
  Sparsely settled even before, and emptied on Pulura's warning. Now fiendish marsh giants hold
  Dyinglight, hezrou dredge the fen for sunken relics, and things called bowel worms live in the
  water. It is the coldest part of the Wound.
- **The Sarkorian Steppe** — the far west: open, flat, dry, and almost featureless, once grazing,
  crossed by mammoth and woolly rhinoceros and by the raiding war-bands of the **Mammoth Lords**,
  who have always thought of it as theirs and who push the border east a mile at a time and lose
  it again. The **Shudderwood**, the one great forest left, fills its southern corner, and the
  fey there hate everyone now.

**The rivers.** The **West Sellen** is the eastern boundary and the road the crusade came in
by; everything east of it is Mendev and everything west of it is this. The **Ahari** runs under
Drezen's walls. The **Sarkora** still flows, south-west of the rift; the **Rolling River** and
the **Isk** do not, and their dry beds are roads now. The **Moutray**, along the Ustalav border,
carried wardstones once. The **Gray Road** west of Drezen is a dry riverbed named for what walks
it.

**Who rules.** Nothing, and everything. The Wound is a loose coalition of demonic warlords, each
holding a fief by force or by guile, united only in wanting Golarion to suffer. The greatest is
the balor **Khorramzadeh**, the **Storm King**, who rules Iz and took Kenabres. Next was the
marilith **Aponavisius**, who took Drezen in 4638 and held it until Rova; she is not in it now,
and is somewhere. The most respected is the witch **Areelu Vorlesh**, who held Undarin, who is
said to have had a hand in opening the Wound, and whose name is behind the corruption of the
wardstones. The lilitu **Minagho** kept Raliscrad. Under them, the **Templars of the Ivory
Labyrinth** — Baphomet's secret society, whose cells hide inside respectable families in cities
across the world and who have infiltrated crusader orders before — and the cults of **Deskari**,
Lord of the Locust Host, whose green-and-red locust wings are the sign of the enemy.

**Humans in the Wound.** More than you would think. Cultists and mercenaries who serve the fiends;
chattel kept for labour, food or sacrifice; the willing workers of Raliscrad; the free folk of
Gundrun; the changed people of Sabertooth Vale and Shadow Spring; the enslaved miners of Iz, who
broke every production record under the whip; and the thousands who lived in occupied Drezen,
among whom **Fenna Tusk**, a half-orc girl of seventeen now with the expedition, was born. A
character born inside the Wound is possible. Ask Matt.

**What you meet on the road.** Babau, brimoraks, vrocks, nabasu, shadow demons; derakni in the
sky; vescavors, colony-swarms that eat metal and skin; plague steeds on the plains between
Storasta and Undarin; urannags, midnight morels, the fungus called Shax's beard; ghouls in the
chapels; the winter wraiths of the Crown of the World, raised from Drezen's own dead. And the
crusade's own losses: seventy-five years of names on the charts, every one of them somebody's.

## 7. Drezen — the city you are standing in

**What it was.** A citadel-city raised in the hubris of the First Crusade, on the eastern arm of
the Wounded Lands, to be the crusade's forward fortress. In 4638 it was betrayed from within by
**Staunton Vhane**, a dwarf paladin turned, and taken by **Aponavisius**; a wave of raw chaos
washed over it and turned most of its people into beast-things that ate each other, and when the
slaughter stopped the corpses were carted to the ice cliffs of the Crown of the World, and from
them rose the winter wraiths. **Saint Argil**, the Pauper Sorcerer, died on the walls covering
the flight of those who got out. The ***Sword of Valor***, the banner of the crusade, was lost
here. The shrines of Iomedae and Sarenrae were made into mockeries of themselves. For
seventy-five years — three generations — it was a demon city of some seven thousand, and the
chronicle calls it the first great wound of the crusade.

**How it was taken.** By a hundred volunteers and four mythic nobodies, in one week of Rova: the
courtyard on the 8th, the sally port and the Templar's Court on the 9th, three days on the first
floor of the citadel, Staunton Vhane dead and buried on the 12th and his glaive unmade in the
forge, and the dungeons on the 13th, where the banner was. **Lupenor** wrecked the enemy's
catapults during the siege. **Kamilo Dann**, who held a dozen wounded in a stone gaol at Vilareth
Ford until the ford was retaken, is one of many small heroes of that fortnight, and yours can be
another.

**Who runs it now.**

- **Irabeth Tirabade** — half-orc paladin of the Eagle Watch, Governor. Held Kenabres together
  with her bare hands and was released by the Queen to ride north.
- **Sir Aldrich Thorne** — the Queen's steward, sent the day after the fall.
- **Aron Kir** — former spy, now Warden of Drezen; keeps the citadel's watch and its secrets.
- **Horgus Gwerm** — a Kenabres noble who counted his gold in the rubble and now counts the city's
  grain; blunt, unsentimental, and the man to see about supply.
- **Anevia Tirabade** — the Governor's wife and the crusade's agent; her spies map the west.
- **Joran Vhane** — Staunton's kinsman, a dwarven master smith, surrendered in the citadel and
  now a penitent who dreams of relighting the forge beneath Drezen for good ends.

**Where things are.**

- **The citadel** — the fortress on the height: the sally port, the Templar's Court, the tiers
  and dungeons the four cleared. The crusade's garrison lives here.
- **Iomedae's Preservers** — **Harlock**'s order: five squads of paladins and clerics sworn to
  hold the city. **Elara Dawnstrider**, a paladin who renounced her noble family, holds the order
  while he is west and calls its recruits; the trials are three days long. **Aldwin
  Brightblade**, once a Kenabres blacksmith, is one of them.
- **Lupenor's Market** — the trading house **Lupenor** founded. **Mira Thistledance**, a half-elf
  bard, keeps it and screens every trader who wants a stall; **Silas Thorne** sells cloth there;
  **Abner Suthi** — never "Avner" — trades honey and sweets and once died on a spike above a lava
  lake and was brought back. The Market hires people with no company behind them for work it does
  not want traced to itself, and does not apologise for it.
- **The temple of Sarenrae** — **Varic**'s foundation, built and funded by him. Its head priest,
  **Rothin Vald**, is two days west keeping the reopened Hidden Temple.
- **The temple of the united faiths** — across the way, **Sosiel Vaenic**'s: a priest of Shelyn,
  charged with building one house for all the good gods of the crusade.
- **The shrine of Erastil** in the southern quarter — reopened by **Jeskar Hinton**, a young
  priest who came with the first refugees, and found desecrated once already. He and his
  hunters are away west; the shrine is not.
- **The Redeemers' camp** — the four's own followers, an old guard from Kenabres and a newer wave
  since. It looks like a festival collided with a refugee camp and decided to stay: bright cloth
  strung between posts, a kitchen and a dining hall that were built toward each other from two
  ends and met at the wrong angle, children chasing chickens, someone juggling badly. **Tilda
  Merrow**, an aging adept, keeps them from dying of their own enthusiasm; **Sir Brannic Vayne**,
  who is not really a knight but has beautiful handwriting, keeps the lists.
- **The crusaders' lines** — neat rows of identical tents, and the merchants' stalls in their
  orderly rows beside them.
- **The refugees** — from Kenabres and the lost settlements, still arriving.
- **The Southshore** — a quarter of scrap yards and rope-walks that spent seventy-five years
  under demon rule and has not been searched. **Cinder Row** runs through it. This is where the
  work is.

**The roads.** East and south, the road down to **Kenabres**: **Keeper's Canyon**, where the road
turns toward Drezen and where two armies camped; **Vilareth Ford**, the crossing on the road up
from Kenabres; about a week's march for a column, less for a rider. West, the road to the
**Hidden Temple** — two days on foot, with nothing on it. Beyond that, the **Gray Road**, the
**Weeping Hills**, the lava country, and everything the Marchlands map on the crusade's site
shows. North, above the city, the **Lake Lost to the Sun**. A hex on the Marchlands chart is
twelve miles, or a hard day's march.

**What Drezen feels like.** A garrison learning to be a town. Acid-scarred stone. Two temples
facing each other across a street, both newer than the season. More buildings than people, and
nobody sure what is in most of them. Every expedition west leaves by the western gate and is
counted home through the same arch — battered, and short of somebody.

## 8. Building the bio — prompts

Answer the ones that fit. Three or four good answers make a character; you do not need all of
them.

**Where were you on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus, 4713?** Everyone in Mendev knows where they
were. It is this generation's "the day the wardstone cracked."

**Which Mendev are you from?** Native, crusader's-child, Sarkorian, or none of the above (a
tiefling of the Wound; a mongrelman; a foreigner off the last boat). What does your family think
of the other two?

**How did you get to Drezen?** Some of the ways the table already has:

1. **With the hundred.** You marched out of Kenabres on the 30th of Arodus with the four. You
   were at Vilareth Ford or Keeper's Canyon. You are one of the volunteers who lived.
2. **With the steward.** You came up with Sir Aldrich Thorne's party on the 14th of Rova, or with
   the relief and supply that has followed.
3. **On a supply wagon.** Conscripted in Nerosyan, or hired on in Egede, or a stowaway. The
   wagons have been rolling since Rova.
4. **With the refugees.** From Kenabres, from a farm the demons burned, from Valas's Gift.
5. **A deserter.** From a crusader company, a mercenary band, or the inquisition. Drezen is the
   one place nobody checks papers.
6. **A pilgrim.** You came for the banner, or the ballad, or the four themselves.
7. **A low templar.** You came for the pay, and there is going to be pay.
8. **Home.** You are Sarkorian, and these are the first walls on this side of the river a Kellid
   could sleep behind in three generations.
9. **You never left.** You were born in occupied Drezen. (Talk to Matt.)
10. **You came to sell something.** Mira Thistledance will want to know what.

**Who do you already know?** Any of the Drezen citizens in §7, at one line's worth: you sold
Horgus Gwerm a wagon; you took Elara Dawnstrider's three-day trial and failed it; Sosiel Vaenic
buried your brother; Tilda Merrow set your arm. The crusade's chronicle has the full entries for
each; the one-line version is all you need to claim.

**What do you believe?** Iomedae, and how much; the Dawnflower; Shelyn; Desna; Erastil; Abadar;
Pharasma; Gorum; Cayden Cailean; the Green Faith; the clan's god; nothing, since Armasse. What is
the gesture you make when you pass the temples?

**What do you carry?** A zoic fetish. A skull in a box. A banner you did not earn. A banner you
did. A sword-and-sun badge on a coat that is not yours. A relic of the pirate days. A brewery
token. Twelve years' service and a scar you explain differently every time.

**What did the Third Crusade do to your family?** If you are native Mendevian or Sarkorian, the
answer is probably not "nothing."

**What does the word "wardstone" mean to you now?**

**What do you want from Drezen?** Pay. A wall. A name. A grave to visit. A god to find. A way
west. A way to never go west again.

### Naming by people

- **Native Mendevian** — Iobarian roots; sturdy, slightly Slavic-sounding. *Hulrun Shappok,
  Irabeth Tirabade, Anevia, Sosiel Vaenic, Nestrin Alodae, Eterrius Sunnestier, Kamilo Dann,
  Trudean Delashaw, Jeskar Hinton, Tilda Merrow.*
- **Crusader stock** — southern names from anywhere: Taldan (*Galfrey, Aron Kir, Aldrich
  Thorne, Brannic Vayne, Dyre, Marcovina*), Chelaxian, Andoren, Varisian. A family name that is
  a place ("of Lauterbury") is a knightly affectation.
- **Sarkorian / Kellid** — short and hard: *Dron, Gurog, Kronug, Belka, Inkit, Yala*; the clan
  name over the given name; a longer, stranger name for the notable: *Uloric Dziergas, Domora
  Hume, Khraigorr Half-Face, Tarnshiav, Widowknife, Farheaven.*
- **Mongrelmen of Neathholm** — single, plain: *Lann, Chusul, Opoli, Chyrrik.*
- **Drezen-born** — whatever the demons let them be called. *Fenna Tusk* is the one example.

## 9. Reserved by the chronicle — do not claim without asking

These belong to the crusade's story, and a new character who takes one of them will collide with
it:

- **The Seeker's Spiral, and the Riftwardens' cohorts of marked children.** Rabiah carries it;
  so does one other. There are no more.
- **The ritual in the Molten Scar, and what was done to Harlock at fifteen.**
- **Terendelev's scales**, the four silver scales carried since the caverns under Kenabres.
- ***Radiance***, ***Solemn Hour***, ***Battle Hymn***, ***Soul Shear***, the ***Sword of
  Valor***, and Yaniel who carried Radiance before.
- **Being in the Gray Garrison** with the four on the 23rd of Arodus. You may have been in the
  Defender's Heart when they came back. You were not up the stairs.
- **Kin to Irabeth, Anevia, Aravashnial, Staunton or Joran Vhane, Galfrey, Hulrun.** Ask first.
- **Arueshalae**, the redeemed succubus, and anything the demons are hunting in the west.
- **The Ivory Sanctum**, Xanthir Vang, and the enemy's intelligence out of Vhane's journals.

Spellings the table has settled, against the transcripts and sometimes against Paizo:
**Chyrrik** (and she is female); **Abner** Suthi; **Aponavisius**; **Jeskar** Hinton; **Lenne**
Marsh; **Deren** Ashfall; **Adara Seln**; **Selyse Avelia**; **Vorimeraak** (female). Use the
chronicle's.
