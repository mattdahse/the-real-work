# Where You Come From — A Character Bio Helper

*The Real Work — the lands around Drezen, for anyone writing a character who is standing in the
city in the autumn of 4713 AR and has not yet worked out why*

This is a handout for building a backstory. It is not a chapter, not a rulebook, and not a
promise that any of it will still be true by Kuthona. It gathers what passes for fact about the
country around **Drezen** — the Worldwound, old Sarkoris, Mendev and its capital Nerosyan — from
Paizo's published setting and from the crusade's own chronicle at
[The Fifth Crusade](https://mattdahse.github.io/the-fifth-crusade/), and it stops at the present
day, because the present day is quite enough.

**Which source wins.** Where Paizo and the chronicle differ, the chronicle governs at this table:
its spellings, its dates, its people. Where the chronicle is silent, Paizo's setting stands. Where
both are silent, make something up and tell Matt, so that it can be written down and later
contradicted by a dice roll.

**How to use it.** Skim §1 so you know what everyone in the city knows. Then read the one or two
sections that match where your character is from, and pick from the prompts in §8. Do not read
the whole thing at once. Nobody in Drezen has that kind of time, and neither do you.

**A note on the general situation.** The Worldwound is the place where the gods' great plan for
the north was found to have a hole in it, and the demons came through the hole. A hundred and
seven years of crusading have established the following: that walls fall, that stones crack, that
saints die on the walls covering the retreat of people who will not remember their names, and
that every pillar of law and virtue erected in this country has so far been knocked over by
something that thought it was funny. This is not a reason to despair. It is a reason to get paid
in advance.

## 1. The moment you are standing in

It is **Neth, 4713 AR** — late autumn, with the first hard frosts on the Marchlands and the
southern horizon lit orange after dark, which the locals have stopped mentioning. Everyone in
Drezen, whoever they are, carries these facts, mostly in the way one carries a bad tooth:

- **Kenabres fell on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus** — Iomedae's own feast day, some three months
  ago, which tells you something about how closely she was watching. The Storm King came over the
  walls, the silver dragon **Terendelev** died in the sky above the city, the great bridge went
  down, and the wardstone in the Kite was shattered. The city that had been the crusade's northern
  wall for a century fell in an afternoon. Prelate **Hulrun**, who had spent fifty years burning
  people to keep the demons out, was among the first the demons killed. There is a lesson in that
  and nobody has agreed on what it is.
- **The wardstones are gone.** A week later, at the **Gray Garrison**, four nobodies out of the
  rubble broke the corrupted fragment before the enemy could turn it against the army gathering
  at the border, and every wardstone along the West Sellen went dark with it. The border that
  held for seventy-five years is now a river and nothing more. The power the stones held did not
  go out of the world; it went into the four nobodies, which is either a miracle or a clerical
  error, and the songs have chosen miracle.
- **The four** are **Rabiah**, a red-haired sorcerer of sixteen who keeps herself spotless in a
  country made of ash; **Harlock Greyforge**, a half-orc of Iomedae who carries a sword that
  talks, and listens to it; **Lupenor Celest**, an elven archer of Desna; and **Varic Sarian**, a
  half-elf priest of the Dawnflower who has taken up hitting things. The table calls them
  **Rabiah's Redeemers**. Mendev calls them the Wardstone's Champions, and there is a ballad, and
  it is sung in every tavern between here and Egede by people who could not point to Drezen on a
  map, whether the four like it or not.
- **Queen Galfrey rode north** to Kenabres herself, and sent the four to Drezen with a hundred
  volunteers who privately believed they were riding to their deaths. Most of them were wrong,
  which is the nearest thing to good news this century has produced. They marched out on the
  30th of Arodus, took **Vilareth Ford** back on the road, declined to walk into **Keeper's
  Canyon** in the dark, and came within sight of Drezen's walls on the 6th of Rova.
- **Drezen changed hands on Fire Day, the 13th of Rova, 4713.** The citadel that fell to the
  demons in 4638 is a crusader city again, for now. The ***Sword of Valor***, the banner lost when
  it fell, was carried out of the dungeons beneath it, flawless after seventy-five years in demon
  hands, which is more than can be said for anyone else who spent time there. **Staunton Vhane**,
  the dwarf who betrayed the city three generations ago, is buried here, and the ground has not
  complained yet.
- **Sir Aldrich Thorne** came from the Queen the next day — by teleportation, with two guards —
  saw the city passed to **Irabeth Tirabade** to govern, and left the same day, declining to stay
  for so much as a meal. He has not been in Drezen since. Draw your own conclusions about what he
  smelled. The crusade holds the walls and the citadel. It does not yet hold every quarter, and
  the quarters it does not hold have noticed.
- **The four are away in the west**, days out of contact, and what they are doing there is not
  common knowledge. The cohorts they left behind are holding the city on a thread, and hiring.
  You are the thread.

If your character has been in Drezen more than a week, they have also heard: that there is a
thousand-platinum bounty posted by the demons themselves on a succubus somewhere in the west,
which is more than the crusade has ever offered for anything; that a shrine of Sarenrae two days
down the western road has been reopened and is being kept by a priest named **Rothin Vald**, who
is either brave or has not been told; that the **South Bank** has not been searched; and that
**Horgus Gwerm**, the quartermaster, is browbeating the crown for grain, and losing.

## 2. Mendev — the crusader state

**The land.** Mendev is the northern end of the continent, where the map runs out of ideas:
snow-covered mountains, icy steppe, and the great evergreen **Estrovian Forest** across its
centre and south, which nobody goes into. North, the ice cliffs of the Crown of the World. East,
across the forest, the **Lake of Mists and Veils**, which every crusader crossed to get here and
which many of them wish they had not. South, across the rivers, **Numeria** — a harsh country of
savages and alien metal that Mendevians want nothing to do with, a sentiment Numeria returns.
West, the **West Sellen River**, and on its far bank the Worldwound. There is almost no arable
land. The country imports nearly everything, and for a century the crusade has been fed by boats:
crusaders and grain coming up the Sellen and across the lake, arriving weekly at **Egede**, in
roughly that order of usefulness.

**Three kinds of Mendevian.** Nobody in Mendev is just "a Mendevian." Ask which, and then ask
what they think of the other two, and then stand back.

- **The natives.** Descended from Iobarian exiles, which is to say the descendants of people
  who were thrown out of somewhere else, semi-nomadic once, and culturally cousins to the
  Sarkorians across the river. Many still keep the old **animist** faith or the **Green Faith**,
  and quietly, because the crusaders have treated them as second-class for a hundred years and
  in the Third Crusade burned them for heresy. A native Mendevian character has a grandmother
  who remembers who informed on whom, and a grandmother's list is longer than the crown's.
- **The crusaders and their children.** From Cheliax, Isger, Andoran, Taldor and everywhere
  else that had people it wanted rid of. The first wave were the finest people of the south:
  clerics, paladins, knights who sold their lands to come, and they are mostly dead. By the
  Fourth Crusade the boats were bringing mercenaries, dispossessed nobles, wandering companies,
  political undesirables and men let out of prison on condition they went north, and by now
  these outnumber the pious. The southern governments discovered that a holy war is a splendid
  place to send your problems, and that the demons will do the paperwork. A crusader's-child
  character can be third-generation Mendevian and still be called a foreigner by the natives, on
  whom their grandfather may have informed.
- **The Sarkorians.** Kellid refugees of the lost kingdom, three generations in exile in a
  country that burned some of them for being homesick in the wrong dialect. See §5. In Nerosyan
  they have a quarter of their own along the western wall, the **Fire Circle**, with shops and
  lodgings kept in the old style and the western wall between them and home. Others took the
  caves of **Saresp Mountain** in the far east, or scattered into Numeria, Ustalav and beyond.

And among them: **tieflings** in unusual numbers (Mendev is home to many asura-spawn, and the
Wound breeds pitborn, and the crusade has never worked out how to feel about fighting demons
beside their grandchildren); **dwarves** whose ancestors fled the lost Sky Citadel of
**Jormurdun** with a royal claim and pieces of a magic key, and who have mostly come down in the
world, many living poor in the Fleshward slum of Chesed on the lake, a royal claim being worth
exactly what it sounds like; halflings; half-elves; and beneath Kenabres, the **mongrelmen**, the
warped descendants of First Crusade soldiers whom the Wound changed and whom their own comrades
then tried to kill, on the grounds that the change was probably their fault.

**Rule.** Mendev is a monarchy under **Queen Galfrey**, the Sword of Iomedae — a Taldan woman
who looks to be in her middle forties and is over a hundred years old, held there by the sun
orchid elixir and by not having time to age. She was crowned in 4601 as a paladin of Aroden,
before there was a Worldwound; five years later Aroden died and the Worldwound opened, and she
retrained herself as Iomedae's paladin with the brisk practicality of a woman whose first god
had let her down. She is the one who ordered the retreat from Sarkoris in 4638 so that the
wardstones could be raised, and she has been hated for it by the descendants of people it kept
alive. Her word is law within Nerosyan's walls. Beyond them, every commander of every city, fort
and army interprets her commands a little differently, and some take more liberty than others,
and the Queen has never had the spare soldiers to do anything about it. Mendev has lived under
martial law so long that no one remembers anything else, and the few who do are not asked. Until
this summer, the second most powerful person in the kingdom was **Prelate Hulrun Shappok** of
Kenabres, the old inquisitor whose witch-burnings as a young man started the Third Crusade, and
who only in his last years began to understand the shame he had brought on the faith. He was
working on it. Then a demon killed him. So it goes.

**Faith.** **Iomedae**, the Inheritor, is the crusade. Her white-and-gold banner is the one all
crusaders nominally fight under, her cathedral is the centre of Nerosyan, and her holy day
**Armasse** is the day Kenabres fell, which her clergy have not found a way to explain and have
stopped trying. She inherited her portfolio from Aroden, who died without warning on the day he
was supposed to come back; she is the god of a promise that was not kept, and she has kept it
better than he did, which is a low bar. Beside her: the remnants of Aroden's church, who began
the First Crusade to have something to do; **Sarenrae**, whose priests march with the columns
and heal what the columns leave; **Shelyn**, **Desna**, **Erastil**, **Abadar**, **Pharasma**,
who at least is guaranteed to show up eventually. **Gorum** has grown popular in the barracks of
Egede for obvious reasons, and **Cayden Cailean**'s faithful have a whole neighbourhood in
Nerosyan, for the other obvious reasons. The Green Faith and the animist ways of the natives
survive, and are looked at sideways, and have learned to look back. Every Mendevian, pious or
not, knows the gesture: fingers to the lips, then pressed to the sword-pommel, as one crosses
oneself before something sacred. It costs nothing and it has never once worked, and everyone
does it anyway. That is religion in Mendev.

**Orders and outfits.** A crusader belongs to something, or pretends to, and after a hundred
years the distinction has worn thin:

- **The Eagle Watch** — the crusaders of Kenabres. **Irabeth Tirabade** commanded them at the
  Defender's Heart; **Anevia**, her wife, served among them; Harlock was on the eve of his oath
  to them when the city fell, and has since found that the oath came with a sword and the sword
  came with opinions.
- **The Order of Heralds** — the knightly order that curbed the Third Crusade's purges and turned
  the war back outward. Still the conscience of the crusade, which is a small job with a large
  title.
- **The Riftwardens** — a secretive society of planar scholars who want the Worldwound *closed*,
  not merely held, based in Kenabres's **Librarium of the Broken Black Wing**, which has since
  partly collapsed, so the name was prophetic. **Aravashnial**, the elven wizard blinded in the
  fall of Kenabres and since healed by the Queen's own priests, is one. Their mark is the
  Seeker's Spiral, and it is not a thing to give a new character without asking; it has been
  known to attract attention.
- **Iomedae's Preservers** — the newest of the orders, founded this autumn by **Harlock
  Greyforge** and headquartered in Drezen: five squads of paladins and clerics sworn to hold the
  city, and a name that is either optimistic or a dare.
- **The Kenabres Wolves** — the spies and scouts, **Aron Kir**'s people. He is Warden of Drezen
  now, which is what happens to spies who survive.
- **The Order of Holy Sentinels** — guardians of the ***Demonscope*** at **Clydwell Keep**, the
  greatest of the border forts. Every fort on the old wardstone line is named for a dead general,
  which was meant as an honour and reads as a warning.
- **The knightly orders** — the Emerald Sword, the Flaming Lance, the Rampant Dragon, the
  Sunrise Sword, the Guild of Diggers and Sappers, and dozens more. Some are ancient. Some were
  invented on the boat, because the heralds of Iomedae cannot check every newcomer's claim to a
  title, and everyone knows it, and the heralds know everyone knows it, and the titles are used
  anyway, because a lie everyone agrees on is what a title is.
- **The Low Templars** — the crusade's own name for its mercenaries, sellswords and adventurers
  who fight for pay or plunder rather than faith. Not an insult, exactly. Not a compliment. The
  crusade's honest opinion of the people who now make up most of it.
- **The Pathfinder Society** — present in Nerosyan since the Queen gave them the Starrise Spire
  in 4707, and sending expeditions into the Wound disguised as mercenary companies, on the theory
  that the demons will treat scholars worse than they treat sellswords, which is probably true.
- **The inquisitors** — Hulrun's people. Fewer than they were, and quieter since Kenabres, but
  the cleansing fire still glows strongest there, and fire is not known for its judgement.

**Banners.** Any crusader stronghold is a riot of colour: streamers and flags from hundreds of
clans, counties, kingdoms and families competing for room, most of them representing places that
would not take their bearers back. The banners that matter are the ones earned on the field, and
they take the form of Iomedae's own symbols — the **sword**, the **shield**, or the **sunburst**
— and are granted to soldiers of any rank, which is the single most democratic institution in
Mendev and consists of a piece of cloth.

**Speech.** Mendevians speak **Taldane** (Common); the natives and the Sarkorians also speak
**Hallit**, the Kellid tongue, which has no written form and so has never been used to sign
anything. Two words of Mendevian slang worth knowing: a **gaffle** or a **rip** is a scam. A
country that needs two words for it has a lot of them.

**The wardstones, and their absence.** For seventy-five years the border was a line of
rune-carved menhirs along the West Sellen and the Moutray, infused with **nexovar** from the
hills of the Egelsee, maintained by daily prayer and a yearly ritual, and housed in a chain of
forts. Nothing chaotic or evil could pass between them, and no teleportation could cross the
line. It was the crusade's one unarguable achievement, and it was built by the crusade that was
declared a failure, and it is gone since Arodus. A character who served on the wardstone line —
a priest who kept the prayers, a soldier in one of the named forts — has lost the whole meaning
of their posting in a single week, and has probably kept the posting, because the pay still
comes.

## 3. Nerosyan — the Diamond of the North

The capital, and the crusade's fortress. Before the Worldwound it was a run-down river town of
pirates; the First Crusade widened its streets, walled it, filled it with soldiers and gave it a
holy purpose, and the piracy never entirely left, it simply got a uniform. Population about
sixty-five thousand, nearly all human, nearly all certain.

**Where it sits.** At the junction where the **Egelsee River** runs into the northernmost branch
of the **West Sellen**. The Egelsee is the city's only clean water; the Sellen comes down too
close to the Wound to drink, and the city is built between a river it can drink and a river it
cannot, which is Mendev in miniature. Everything outside the walls has been burned flat by
demonic raids, and by the defenders, on purpose, so that nothing approaches unseen. Nobody now
remembers which fires were whose.

**How it is built.** A diamond between the two rivers, so that every wall has water in front of
it. The outer walls are twenty feet thick, crenellated and towered, and the wall-tops are
*roofed* — sloped roofs cut with arrow slits, because the enemy flies, and it took the crusade a
while to accept that. At the centre stands the **Cruciform Cathedral** of Iomedae, and the
avenues run wide and straight from it so that a sortie can reach any wall in minutes, and so
that the cathedral can see everything, which comes to the same thing. Four great towers hold the
points: the **Battle Tower** in the north, the most heavily fortified structure in Mendev; the
**Woundward Tower**, which holds the city's wardstone and its best archers and engines against
attack from the air; the **Confluence Tower**, guarding the rivers and sheltering Crusader's
Quay; and the **Egelsee Tower**, the cavalry's staging ground. The **Sellen Docks** are stone
quays cut into the bedrock, designed to survive anything but a bad harvest. Above it all burns
the **Starrise Spire**, older than the walls, with a ball of light floating over it that has not
gone out in centuries — a beacon to the crusade and a warning to the Wound, and the Wound has had
a century to get used to it.

**The districts.** Battle, Egelsee, Confluence and Woundward are the four quarters, named with
the imagination one expects of a military government. Within them: **Pilgrim's Rest**, where the
endless stream of Iomedaean pilgrims lodges and is relieved of its money; **Bannerbright**, where
the knightly and clerical orders keep their houses and their feuds; the **Two Cs**, the
neighbourhood of Cayden Cailean's faithful, which is where the taverns are and therefore where
the crusade actually lives; the **Fire Circle** along the western wall, where the Sarkorian
refugees keep their shops and eating houses in the old style, facing a wall, facing home. On the
east side, **Hank the Alerunner's** hideout is a tavern full of relics of the city's pirate days,
patronised by the pirates' grandchildren in the city guard. **Ancuin's Brewery** has been the
crusaders' favourite for a hundred years and is the institution with the best record in Mendev.

**Who runs it.** The Queen, from her palace, counselled by an angel named **Opaline**, who has
presumably seen the whole plan and is not sharing. Around her: **Lord Inquisitor Casori**;
**Captain Jaspar** of the Crusader Heralds; Venture-Captain **Jorsal of Lauterbury** of the
Pathfinders. The city is law-abiding, beautiful in its design, and entirely clear about what it
is for, which is more than can be said for the war.

**What has happened lately.** For years the city has been harried by things that come up the
rivers — merrow, scrags, grindylows, the water-things that serve the hezrou that rules ruined
Storasta downriver, whose idea of a joke is to make the crusade's river run the wrong way — and
there are stories of a larger siege this year, turned back with the Pathfinders in the thick of
it, which the Pathfinders tell. The wagons of conscripts and supply have been rolling north to
Kenabres and on to Drezen since Rova, and the conscripts have been rolling off them.

**Bio hooks from Nerosyan.** A dock-hand on the Sellen quays who has unloaded a hundred boatloads
of heroes and watched none come back down. A cathedral acolyte who has never been outside the
walls and believes everything, so far. A Two Cs tavern brawler with a holy excuse. A child of the
Fire Circle who speaks Hallit at home and Taldane in the street and lies in both. A brewery
drayman, the most respected trade in the city. A conscript caught stealing bread and put on a
supply wagon, who slipped away the moment it passed Drezen's gate (that is how **Tam**, the
Redeemers' water-carrier, arrived, and Tam is not the only one, and nobody is counting). A clerk
in the palace who saw the Queen's letter from Drezen before it was read aloud, and saw what was
cut.

## 4. Kenabres and the border — what fell, and what stands

**Kenabres** was the crusade's northern city: twelve thousand people on graduated tiers rising
from the eastern plain, its western edge a cliff above the West Sellen, its water drawn from the
river below, its back to the Wound and its face to the kingdom that had sent it there. **Old
Kenabres** at the top held the oldest families and the administration, which were the same
families; the **Ring District** around it was the rest of the original town; **New Kenabres**
was built after the Worldwound opened, for refugees of the lost settlements, and the **Gate
District** ten years later when New Kenabres overflowed, because the settlements kept getting
lost. The **Truestone Quarry**, ten miles east, sent stone weekly to the Southgate to reinforce
walls that fell in an afternoon. The road to Egede is 225 miles and ten days by caravan, skirting
the Estrovian Forest past the **Chapel of the Argent Shield**, a fortified temple that was
Aroden's and is now Iomedae's, the way most things that were Aroden's are now somebody else's.

It was also the crusade's conscience and its shame, which in Kenabres were the same building:
the seat of Hulrun's inquisition, where the Third Crusade's fires burned hottest, and where the
generals **Dyre** and **Marcovina** fought the Prelate for every soldier he wanted kept home to
hunt heretics instead of demons, on the grounds that heretics were easier to find. The **Kite**
held the first wardstone ever raised. The **Cathedral of Saint Clydwell** stood in
the old town. The **Blackwing Library** held the crusade's greatest store of learning on the
demons and the planes, all of which was consulted, none of which helped.

**What is left.** Half the population — about six and a half thousand — in a city split by
rifts, its cathedral and the Kite destroyed, the library partly collapsed, and the Queen's own
appointee in the Prelate's chair, which is at least still a chair. The **Defender's Heart**, the
fortified inn where Irabeth held the survivors together, is the heart of what remains, and it
was an inn, and that is the most honest thing about the crusade: when the cathedral fell, the
people went to the pub. The assault tore open layers of tunnels under the city, and out of them
came the mongrelmen of **Neathholm** — a hamlet of a few dozen on an island in an underground
lake, under its one-eyed chief **Chusul**, descended from First Crusade soldiers whom the Wound
changed and whom the crusade then tried to kill for the inconvenience. They have lived under the
holiest city in the north for ninety years and the holiest city never once looked down.
**Chyrrik**, who marched with the four, is one of them, and there are mongrelmen in the common
room of the Defender's Heart now who did not exist in anyone's world three months ago, and are
being served, mostly.

**The road north.** **Valas's Gift**, a village of a couple of hundred on the road north of
Kenabres, named for a holy man and its red-water springs, and the springs were red before the
war. The **Vineyard Hills Waystation**, a day's ride south-east of the city, a crossroads outpost
well behind the old wardstone line — the last ordinary place the four stood before the march,
and it was attacked anyway. The **Estrovian Forest** itself, where few go willingly: hernes,
stag-horned things that were once trespassing huntsmen and were cursed by druids who then died
of their own curse; witchwargs; a pack of owlbears that may be ghosts and have not been asked;
taiga giants driven in by the opening of the Wound; and the werebear-kin, respected as the only
guides who will take you through, and as the only inhabitants who could eat you and generally
choose not to. **Clydwell Keep** and the named forts of the wardstone line, now guarding a line
that is not there, with full garrisons and a budget. And far off, the cursed ruins of **Icerift
Castle**, about which people do not talk, so you know it is worth asking.

**Egede**, on the lake. The second city, nearly forty thousand, the end of the River Road and the
first Mendevian ground most crusaders ever set foot on, and for a great many of them the last
they enjoyed. Where exhausted soldiers are sent to rest, which means prayer at the temples or the
covert brothels, and the temples know about the brothels and the brothels know about the
temples and both keep the other's hours. Where merchants have made an industry of bankrupting
pilgrims on their way to the war, so that the demons get them pre-stripped. Aroden drowned a
demon lord here once, and Egedens celebrate **Drowning Day** for twenty-four hours in a way
outsiders find disturbing, and Aroden is not here to say whether he minds.

**Bio hooks from Kenabres.** Where were you on Armasse? In the Gate District when the bridge came
down. In the quarry caravan, ten miles out, watching the sky do something the sky should not. In
the cells of the inquisition, which turned out to be the safest place in the city. In the tunnels,
and you did not know the tunnels were there, and neither did the people who built the city on top
of them. Among the refugees sheltering in the Defender's Heart when four filthy strangers walked
back in with the wardstone broken behind them and everyone decided, on no evidence, that this was
good. A city watchman with twelve years' service and a burn scar you explain differently every
time (that is **Sera Vost**; there are others like her). A craftsman's son who followed Harlock
because Harlock was walking in a direction (that is **Deren Ashfall**; there are others like
him). A carpenter who lost workshop, tools and wife in an afternoon and has been very calm about
it (that is **Tobias Runt**). A mongrelman of Neathholm above ground for the first time, finding
it overrated.

## 5. Old Sarkoris — the country that was

For a thousand years before the Worldwound, the land west of the Sellen was **Sarkoris**: a
savage, proud, painted Kellid kingdom of fiercely independent clans, founded in 3672 when a
confederation of tribes broke the frost giants of the Northmounds and took the Northmounds as a
prize, which in hindsight was the mistake. Everything on the crusade's charts west of the river
is Sarkorian — the steadings, the shrines, the holdfasts — because the crusade has never had
anything better to put in their place, and because it turns out you can conquer a country three
times and still be using its maps.

**The people.** **Kellids**: lean, dark-haired, light-brown-skinned, with dark eyes and scars
they are proud of, because a Kellid without scars is thought to have lived too comfortably, and a
Kellid who has lived comfortably is thought to have done it at someone else's expense. They dress
in leather, fur and hide, and wear **zoic fetishes**, little charms of twine, hair and stone that
their druids enchant and that work about as often as prayer. They value strength, bravery and
cunning; they have little patience for compromise, carelessness or weak leaders, which made them
poor subjects and worse allies and the only people in the north who did not lose Sarkoris
politely; they carry only what the clan needs; they respect Ulfen and dwarves as kindred spirits
and distrust magic and machines both, having lived between Irrisen's witches and Numeria's
engines and seen what each does to a neighbour. The **clan** comes before the self, which is
convenient, since most of the clans are gone and the self is what is left. Names are short and
percussive, two syllables or fewer — **Dron**, **Gurog**, **Kronug**; **Belka**, **Inkit**,
**Yala** — and second names are rare, kept for the distinguished or the well-born; a clan name
matters more than either. Sarkorian names of record run longer and stranger: **Uloric Dziergas**
the warlord, **Domora Hume** the god-caller, **Khraigorr Half-Face**, who got the name honestly,
the **Tarnshiavs** of Dyinglight, the **Widowknife** clanhold, the **Farheaven** clan.

**The clans and their rulers.** Sarkoris was never one thing, which is why it took the demons
almost forty years to finish it. Each clan-hold had its **clanliege**; the great cities had their
**witch-wardens**; and warlords rose and fell, mostly fell. The mystics and witches of Sarkoris
were a byword across Avistan, and it was their oration that warned, in the years before 4606, of
ascendant chaos and a thinning between the worlds. They were right about everything. No one
listened, because Aroden was prophesied to return and settle it all, and a prophecy is so much
more restful than a witch.

**God-calling.** The heart of Sarkorian religion, and the only part of it a southerner can
describe without laughing, because it worked. A **god-caller** binds a spirit — the clan's own
patron, usually in the shape of a native beast made strange, a bear with feathers, a wolf with
wings — and calls it into the world, where the clan worships it as their god, and the god,
unlike most, turns up. The forms were consistent across generations; marriages between clans
produced hybrid gods, and presumably arguments. Southern churches call this the worship of false
gods, on the authority of true gods who have not been seen since 4606. The Sarkorians called it
the only religion that ever answered when spoken to. Many of the clan-gods were destroyed or
driven off in the first years of the Wound — the demons found them, which the demons could not
say of Iomedae — and a Sarkorian character of god-calling blood may carry a name for a god no one
has seen in a hundred years, and a grudge on its behalf.

**The old gods.** Besides the clan-spirits: **Pulura**, the Shimmering Maiden, Mistress of the
Stars and the northern lights, honoured at **Dyinglight** with a great ring of idols, and paired
with **Kostchtchie** the demon of cold as the two faces of winter, because the Sarkorians had
noticed that winter has two faces and only one of them is pretty; the **Green Faith**, born in
the Forest of Stones, which is now the Stonewilds, which tells you how that went; **Gorum**, whom
Kellids insist was one of their own and who has not denied it; **Desna**, whom they see not as an
elf but as a Kellid woman on an elk who leads them to game, and who presumably finds the elves'
version as puzzling; **Erastil**; **Pharasma** at Raliscrad, whose crypts are the only thing in
Raliscrad still in order; and names now half-lost — **Alglenweis**, **Dolok Darkfur**, **Kagia**,
**Ristrentho**, the **Stag Mother** of the Forest of Stones, **Sturovenen** the Dragoneagle and
his dawn-feathered children. There was worship of Sarenrae here too, in the old days, in shrines
like the one two days west of Drezen; **Brix Copperfinch**, a gnome scholar of the Dawnflower,
has made a life's study of it, and the study is longer than the worship was. And there was always
Deskari's cult in the Northmounds, driven out by Aroden and never quite gone, because the thing
about locusts is that you never get all of them.

**Huzuris.** The clans preserved the memories of their chiefs and witch-wardens in the skulls of
animals bound to the clan's story — the **huzuri**, in which the clan's soul was held. A clan
that lost its huzuri had lost itself. Some came out with the refugees. Most did not, and so most
of the clans of Sarkoris are, by their own theology, dead, and the people walking around calling
themselves by clan names are ghosts who have not been told. They are surprisingly cheerful about
it.

**The cities.** **Iz**, the capital, a centre of stone and metalwork beside its mines, and the
first to fall; two-thirds of it went into the rift the day it opened, and the remaining third has
been the demons' capital ever since, with embassies. **Undarin**, on the Sarkora where the
Rolling River joined it, the market where the miners of the plateau met the herders of the
steppe — its districts Cliffside, Steppeside and the five holy isles of Gorum's Chain; the demons
found the stockyards useful, and not for cattle. **Storasta**, at the meeting of the Sarkora and
the West Sellen, a shaman-ruled city of groves and gardens, independent for centuries and
annexed in 4000 by its neighbours for its own good; the last city to fall, its sacred circles
held by witches and shamans with druids of Mendev and Numeria beside them, and their last stand
is the ***Song of Sarkoris***, which is very beautiful and did not help. **Dyinglight**, in the
fens of the far north-west, the spiritual heart of the kingdom; the priests of Pulura's
monastery at **Pulura's Fall** received a vision and the city emptied before the demons arrived,
the single instance in this history of a god warning anyone in time, and it was a minor one.
**Raliscrad**, the trade town where the Sarkora met the Isk, abandoned in such haste that the
wealth was left on the tables, where the demons found it and the humans who stayed have been
counting it for them since. **Gundrun**, far to the south-west, destroyed, then rebuilt around
4670 by people who simply came back, and who have survived seventy years in the Wound by the
sound strategy of being beneath notice. **Domora** in the west, where a god-caller named Hume
called a water spirit out of a pool in 4172 to break a Mammoth Lord raid, and taught the art
anew, and the pool is still there and the art is not. **Threshold**, a name at the western lip of
the canyon country, which the old accounts call a prison-tower of the High Cairns where Sarkoris
kept its arcane casters to work under the eyes of the druids; the druids' eyes are no longer on
anything. And under the Wolfscrags, **Jormurdun**, a dwarven Sky Citadel lost since 1600 AR,
whose royal line scattered across the world with pieces of a broken key, so that the family
heirloom is a fragment of something that does not work.

**The diaspora.** The refugees went everywhere, and everywhere had an opinion about it. Into
Mendev, where they are the Fire Circle and the cave-dwellers of Saresp, and where the Third
Crusade's inquisitors treated their animism as demon-worship and burned them for it, thereby
completing the demons' work at the crown's expense. Into Numeria. Into Ustalav, where the locals
call them **leechfolk** and where the northern Shudderwood is haunted by Sarkorians the Ustalavs
murdered, who have at least stayed to make a point. West to the Realm of the Mammoth Lords, who
share their blood and took them in the way one takes in a cousin. A few as far as Iobaria and
Garund, where nobody knows what a Sarkorian is and so nobody minds. And a stubborn handful never
left at all: the folk of Gundrun, the **Tarnshiavs** at Shadow Spring in the fen, the Kellids of
**Sabertooth Vale** (who are not what they were, and would rather you did not ask), and the
humans of **Raliscrad** who chose to work for a demon rather than run, and whose descendants have
never had to wonder whether their government was on their side. Three generations on, "Sarkorian"
means a great-grandmother's stories, a word or two of Hallit, a tattoo pattern, a skull in a box,
and the certainty that the country over the river is *yours* and was taken, and that everyone who
could have given it back has been too busy to try.

**Bio hooks from Sarkoris.** A god-caller's granddaughter with the family god's name and no god,
which puts her ahead of most clerics. A huzuri-keeper whose clan is down to four people, three of
whom are not speaking. A Fire Circle cook who has never seen the country he calls home and cooks
it anyway. A Saresp cave-child. A Kellid raised by crusaders who burned her parents and taught her
to be grateful. A Sarkorian who came home with the crusade's supply wagons because the walls at
Drezen are the first Sarkorian walls a Kellid could sleep behind in seventy-five years, and they
were built by crusaders, and she is trying not to think about that. Someone from Gundrun, who has
lived his whole life inside the Wound and wants to know what all the fuss is about.

## 6. The Worldwound — what Sarkoris became

A hundred and seven years ago a mile-long blight rimmed in black flame opened in the barrows of
the Northmounds, and the land came apart around it: a fan of canyons reaching nearly to every
border, rivers gone into their beds and staying there, and a fire that has not gone out since and
that nobody is trying to put out any more. The name comes from an old ballad's line about a
"wound in the world," which is the last time a poet was useful here. The ground itself shifts in
front of you the nearer you go to the rift; at the edges it settles, mostly, and the crusade has
built its cities on the mostly. Ashstorms come off it. The sky over the centre is poison and
coloured lightning, and the demons who live there find it homely. Even the animals are tainted,
so that the one honest creature in the Wound is the vulture. Every crusader chart of it is a copy
of a Sarkorian chart, and most of the names on it are older than the Wound, because the crusade
has never held anything long enough to name it.

**The five regions**, as the crusade's scholars divide them, from a safe distance:

- **The Wounded Lands** — the east, and the rift itself: what was the Northmounds, now a canyon
  a mile wide and dozens of miles long, full of chaos-stuff and bubbling primordial coagulation,
  which is what the scholars call it when they do not know what it is. **Iz** sits at its heart
  and **Drezen** on its eastern arm, which is to say the crusade's forward citadel is on the same
  limb as the enemy's capital, which is not a metaphor but might as well be. The **Marchlands** —
  badlands, canyon and lava west of Drezen — are what the crusade calls the part of this it has
  had to learn; the name is the table's, not the charts', and the charts have not been asked.
- **The Stonewilds** — the broken high country to the north, once the vast evergreen **Forest
  of Stones**, birthplace of the Green Faith and keeper of its holiest texts. When it fell, a
  faction of its druids chose undeath rather than defeat, which is the sort of thing that seems
  like a good idea at the time, and the **Stonewild Sect** now defends the Stonewilds against
  demon and living alike, having concluded that the living were the problem. The **Petrified
  Library** and **Greengrave Keep** are up there, and nobody in Drezen can say who holds either,
  and nobody in Drezen has volunteered to find out.
- **Riftshadow** — the river country: the Sarkora and its towns, once the most populous part of
  the kingdom, now ruins under demonic barges. **Undarin** at its north end, **Storasta** at its
  south, **Raliscrad** between, and the river running past all three as if nothing had happened.
- **Frostmire** — the frozen north-west: the **Wolfscrags**, whose confusing valleys hold packs
  of dire wolves, and **Frostmire Fen**, huge, frigid marshland broken by sulphurous hot springs,
  the one place in the Wound that smelled like that before the demons came. Sparsely settled even
  before, and emptied on Pulura's warning. Now fiendish marsh giants hold Dyinglight and have
  taken up cannibalism, which even the demons think is a bit much; hezrou dredge the fen for
  sunken relics; and things called bowel worms live in the water, and the name is descriptive. It
  is the coldest part of the Wound, and the demons who were sent there know why.
- **The Sarkorian Steppe** — the far west: open, flat, dry, and almost featureless, once grazing,
  crossed by mammoth and woolly rhinoceros and by the raiding war-bands of the **Mammoth Lords**,
  who have always thought of it as theirs and who push the border east a mile at a time and lose
  it again, and have been doing so for a century with the patience of people who have never
  heard of a crusade. The **Shudderwood**, the one great forest left, fills its southern corner,
  and the fey there hate everyone now, evenly, which is a kind of fairness.

**The rivers.** The **West Sellen** is the eastern boundary and the road the crusade came in by;
everything east of it is Mendev and everything west of it is this, and the river does not care.
The **Ahari** ran under Drezen's walls until the Wound opened; its dry bed now cuts the city in
half, which is the one thing in Drezen the demons did not have to do themselves. The **Sarkora**
still flows, south-west of the rift, out of habit; the **Rolling River** and the **Isk** do not,
and their dry beds are roads now, which is what a river becomes when it gives up. The
**Moutray**, along the Ustalav border, carried wardstones once. The **Gray Road** west of Drezen
is a dry riverbed named for what walks it, and the locals decline to be more specific.

**Who rules.** Nothing, and everything, and it is more stable than Mendev. The Wound is a loose
coalition of demonic warlords, each holding a fief by force or by guile, united only in wanting
Golarion to suffer, which as a political programme has the merit of being achievable. The
greatest is the balor **Khorramzadeh**, the **Storm King**, who rules Iz and took Kenabres and
has been at this for a hundred years without a promotion. Next was the marilith
**Aponavisius**, who took Drezen in 4638 and held it until Rova; she is not in it now, and is
somewhere, and the somewhere is presumably not far. The most respected is the witch **Areelu
Vorlesh**, who held Undarin, who is said to have had a hand in opening the Wound, and whose name
is behind the corruption of the wardstones — respected, note, not obeyed; demons do not obey,
they are merely impressed. The lilitu **Minagho** kept Raliscrad, with a staff. Under them, the
**Templars of the Ivory Labyrinth** — Baphomet's secret society, whose cells hide inside
respectable families in cities across the world and who have infiltrated crusader orders before,
because a crusader order is a group of people who will believe anything said in the right tone
of voice — and the cults of **Deskari**, Lord of the Locust Host, whose green-and-red locust
wings are the sign of the enemy, and whose entire theology is that everything should be eaten.
He is winning. He has been winning since 4606. The crusade's great insight, a century in, is
that he does not seem to be in a hurry, and the reason he is not in a hurry is that he is
enjoying it.

**Humans in the Wound.** More than you would think, and fewer than there were. Cultists and
mercenaries who serve the fiends, and who would tell you that at least their employers keep
their word; chattel kept for labour, food or sacrifice, in that order of preference; the willing
workers of Raliscrad; the free folk of Gundrun; the changed people of Sabertooth Vale and Shadow
Spring; the enslaved miners of Iz, who broke every production record under the whip, which is
the one economic success story of the north; and the thousands who lived in occupied Drezen,
among whom **Fenna Tusk**, a half-orc girl of seventeen, was born, and grew up, and is by all
accounts cheerful, which raises questions. A character born inside the Wound is possible. Ask
Matt. Ask nicely.

**What you meet on the road.** Babau, brimoraks, vrocks, nabasu, shadow demons; derakni in the
sky; vescavors, colony-swarms that eat metal and skin and are not particular about the order;
plague steeds on the plains between Storasta and Undarin; urannags, midnight morels, the fungus
called Shax's beard, which is exactly what it sounds like and grows where it likes; ghouls in the
chapels, where the pews are; the winter wraiths of the Crown of the World, raised from Drezen's
own dead and still, in some sense, on the garrison rolls. And the crusade's own losses:
seventy-five years of names on the charts, every one of them somebody's, and every one of them
marking a place someone was sure was safe.

## 7. Drezen — the city you are standing in

**What it was.** A citadel-city raised in the hubris of the First Crusade, on the eastern arm of
the Wounded Lands, to be the crusade's forward fortress and a monument to the proposition that
the war was as good as won. In 4638 it was betrayed from within by **Staunton Vhane**, a dwarf
paladin turned, because of course it was a paladin, and taken by **Aponavisius**; a wave of raw
chaos washed over it and turned most of its people into beast-things that ate each other, and
when the slaughter stopped the corpses were carted to the ice cliffs of the Crown of the World,
and from them rose the winter wraiths, so that Drezen's dead were put to work before they were
cold. **Saint Argil**, the Pauper Sorcerer, died on the walls covering the flight of those who
got out, and was made a saint for it, which is the crusade's pension scheme. The ***Sword of
Valor***, the banner of the crusade, was lost here. The shrines of Iomedae and Sarenrae were
made into mockeries of themselves, by demons who had clearly studied the originals. For
seventy-five years — three generations — it was a demon city of some seven thousand, and the
chronicle calls it the first great wound of the crusade, and the crusade has spent the
seventy-five years pretending the wound was a scratch.

**How it was taken.** By a hundred volunteers and four mythic nobodies, in one week of Rova: the
courtyard on the 8th, the sally port and the Templar's Court on the 9th, three days on the first
floor of the citadel, Staunton Vhane dead and buried on the 12th and his glaive unmade in the
forge, and the dungeons on the 13th, where the banner was, being kept for reasons the demons
have not explained. **Lupenor** wrecked the enemy's catapults during the siege, which nobody had
thought to do in seventy-five years. **Kamilo Dann**, who held a dozen wounded in a stone gaol at
Vilareth Ford until the ford was retaken, is one of many small heroes of that fortnight, and yours
can be another, on the understanding that "small hero" is a description of the pay.

**Who runs it now.** In the sense that anyone does.

- **Irabeth Tirabade** — half-orc paladin of the Eagle Watch, Governor. Held Kenabres together
  with her bare hands and was released by the Queen to ride north, on the principle that a woman
  who can hold one falling city can be given another.
- **Aron Kir** — former spy, now Warden of Drezen; keeps the citadel's watch and its secrets, and
  has the look of a man who has read your file.
- **Horgus Gwerm** — a Kenabres noble who counted his gold in the rubble and now counts the city's
  grain; blunt, unsentimental, and the man to see about supply, and about being told no.
- **Anevia Tirabade** — the Governor's wife and the crusade's agent; her spies map the west, and
  her wife governs it, and between them the household knows more than the crown.
- **Joran Vhane** — Staunton's kinsman, a dwarven master smith, surrendered in the citadel and
  now a penitent who dreams of relighting the forge beneath Drezen for good ends, which is what
  the last Vhane said he was doing.

**Where things are.** The crusade's chart of the city is the Map tab on this site; the letters
here are its letters. North is to the upper right, and the citadel walls are a little under a
mile from the South Bank, which is further than anyone from the citadel has walked.

- **Citadel Drezen** (A) — the star-fort in its moat on the high ground, inside a pentagonal
  curtain wall with six towers and one gate facing south into the city: the sally port, the
  Templar's Court, the tiers and dungeons the four cleared. The crusade holds it outright, and
  the garrison lives here, behind the walls, where the crusade has always preferred to be.
  Everything the company is paid to do is counted out of this arch and counted back through it,
  and the counting is exact both ways.
- **Iomedae's Preservers** (B) — **Harlock**'s order, walled on the high ground north-east of
  the city: garrison, barracks, chapel, stable, a watchtower over the western approach, and a
  parade ground drilled every morning by people who are not yet sure what they are drilling for.
  Five squads of paladins and clerics sworn to hold the city. **Elara Dawnstrider**, a paladin
  who renounced her noble family and has not said what they did, holds the order while he is west
  and calls its recruits; the trials are three days long and most of the recruits are shorter.
  **Aldwin Brightblade**, once a Kenabres blacksmith, is one of them, and misses the forge.
- **Varic's temple compound** (C) — on the eastern ridge, ringed by its own low wall, because
  even the Dawnflower's people have noticed where they are. At the centre, the **temple of
  Sarenrae**, **Varic**'s foundation; its head priest **Rothin Vald** is two days west keeping the
  reopened Hidden Temple, leaving the temple in Drezen with a founder in the west and a head
  priest in the west and a congregation wondering. Across the way, the **temple of the united
  faiths**, **Sosiel Vaenic**'s — a priest of Shelyn, charged with building one house for all the
  good gods of the crusade, on the theory that they will get along better than their clergy do.
  An orphanage, a food bank and an infirmary fill the rest of the ground, which says plainly what
  the compound is for, and what the city is producing.
- **Rabiah's compound** (D) — the ring quarter east of the ridge, wound in circles around the
  old **amphitheatre**, pre-crusade stone and the one place in the city that will hold a crowd,
  which nobody has yet had a reason to test: bazaar, brewery, bathhouse, community
  kitchen, workshop row, and a shrine over a meeting square. Built fast and built crooked — the
  kitchen and the dining hall were begun from two ends by two people and met at the wrong angle,
  and are now called Tuesday's mistake — and the most alive ground in Drezen, which is not the
  compliment it sounds like. The four's own followers live here, an old guard from Kenabres and a
  newer wave since, and the difference is that the old guard has stopped expecting a plan.
  **Tilda Merrow**, an aging adept, keeps them from dying of their own enthusiasm; **Sir Brannic
  Vayne**, who is not really a knight but has beautiful handwriting, keeps the lists, and the
  handwriting is how he got the title.
- **The Celeste Market District** (E) — **Lupenor**'s foundation, packed between the citadel
  wall and the riverbank: vendors under canvas, an auction house, warehouses along the wall,
  three inns, and Lupenor's residence above it all, where she can watch the money. **Mira
  Thistledance**, a half-elf bard, screens every trader who wants a stall and has never yet
  explained her criteria; **Silas Thorne** sells cloth, in a city where everyone's clothes are
  ruined, and is doing very well; **Abner Suthi** — never "Avner" — trades honey and sweets, and
  once died on a spike above a lava lake and was brought back, and now sells sweets, which is
  what a man does with a second life when he has thought about it. The Market hires people with
  no company behind them for work it does not want traced to itself, and does not apologise for
  it, because an apology would be traceable.
- **The shrine of Erastil** in the southern quarter — reopened by **Jeskar Hinton**, a young
  priest who came with the first refugees, and found desecrated once already, which for a shrine
  in Drezen is a good first month.
- **The Ahari riverbed** — dry since the Wound opened, cutting the city in half, crossed by two
  bridges, and a road for anything that would rather not use the gates, and a great many things
  would rather not.
- **The South Bank** (F) — the quarter across the riverbed: scrap yards and rope-walks that
  spent three generations under demon rule and have not been searched, because searching them
  would mean going in. **Cinder Row** runs through it. The crusade holds the walls above it and
  very little of what is under its roofs, and has decided that this counts. This is where the
  work is. This is why you are being paid, and why the pay is what it is.
- **The cemetery** — outside the walls to the west, on the far bank. Older than the occupation,
  and added to steadily since the retaking, and the only district in Drezen with a growing
  population.
- **The crusaders' lines and the refugees** — neat rows of identical tents beside the merchants'
  stalls, and the people out of Kenabres and the lost settlements, still arriving, still being
  told the city is not ready for them, still staying.

**The roads.** East and south, the road down to **Kenabres**: **Keeper's Canyon**, where the road
turns toward Drezen and where two armies camped, one of which is still there in pieces;
**Vilareth Ford**, the crossing on the road up from Kenabres; about a week's march for a column,
less for a rider, less still for whatever is following the rider. West, the road to the **Hidden
Temple** — two days on foot, with nothing on it, and "nothing" is a technical term. Beyond that,
the **Gray Road**, the **Weeping Hills**, the lava country, and everything the Marchlands map on
the crusade's site shows, which the company will not be asked to visit, until it is. North, above
the city, the **Lake Lost to the Sun**, named for the light that does not reach it, so at least
you know what you are getting. A hex on the Marchlands chart is twelve miles, or a hard day's
march, or a comfortable day's flight for the things that fly.

**What Drezen feels like.** A garrison learning to be a town, and the town learning what a
garrison thinks a town is for. Acid-scarred stone. Two temples facing each other across a street,
both newer than the season and both taking bets. More buildings than people, and nobody sure
what is in most of them, and a standing policy of not finding out. Every party that goes west
leaves by the western gate and is counted home through the same arch — battered, and short of
somebody, and the somebody is entered in a ledger, and the ledger is the most complete record
Drezen keeps.

## 8. Building the bio — prompts

Answer the ones that fit. Three or four good answers make a character; you do not need all of
them, and nobody in Drezen has all of them about themselves either.

**Where were you on Armasse, the 16th of Arodus, 4713?** Everyone in Mendev knows where they
were. It is this generation's "the day the wardstone cracked," and the previous generation's
"the day the wardstone cracked" was also the day the wardstone cracked. This is a country with
one story.

**Which Mendev are you from?** Native, crusader's-child, Sarkorian, or none of the above (a
tiefling of the Wound; a mongrelman; a foreigner off the last boat, not yet disillusioned). What
does your family think of the other two? What did your family *do* to the other two?

**How did you get to Drezen?** Some of the ways the table already has, none of them good:

1. **With the hundred.** You marched out of Kenabres on the 30th of Arodus with the four. You
   were at Vilareth Ford or Keeper's Canyon. You are one of the volunteers who lived, which was
   not the plan, and you have not yet adjusted.
2. **With the relief.** You came up with the reinforcement and supply that has followed since
   Rova, which is to say you arrived after the fighting and before the pay.
3. **On a supply wagon.** Conscripted in Nerosyan, or hired on in Egede, or a stowaway. The
   wagons have been rolling since Rova, and the crown has never audited one.
4. **With the refugees.** From Kenabres, from a farm the demons burned, from Valas's Gift. You
   fled a demon-haunted city toward the demons, because that was the direction the road went.
5. **A deserter.** From a crusader company, a mercenary band, or the inquisition. Drezen is the
   one place nobody checks papers, because nobody in Drezen has any.
6. **A pilgrim.** You came for the banner, or the ballad, or the four themselves, and have found
   the banner in a citadel you are not allowed into, the four somewhere in the west, and the
   ballad in every tavern, sung badly.
7. **A low templar.** You came for the pay, and there is going to be pay. The word "going" is
   doing a great deal of work in that sentence.
8. **Home.** You are Sarkorian, and these are the first walls on this side of the river a Kellid
   could sleep behind in three generations, and a crusader built them, and a demon kept them, and
   you are sleeping behind them anyway.
9. **You never left.** You were born in occupied Drezen. (Talk to Matt. Expect questions.)
10. **You came to sell something.** Mira Thistledance will want to know what. So will Aron Kir,
    and he will not ask.

**Who do you already know?** Any of the Drezen citizens in §7, at one line's worth: you sold
Horgus Gwerm a wagon, and he still thinks he was robbed; you took Elara Dawnstrider's three-day
trial and lasted two; Sosiel Vaenic buried your brother and did it beautifully; Tilda Merrow set
your arm, and it was the other arm. The crusade's chronicle has the full entries for each; the
one-line version is all you need to claim, and all they will admit to.

**What do you believe?** Iomedae, and how much, and since when; the Dawnflower; Shelyn; Desna;
Erastil; Abadar, who at least has a contract; Pharasma, who will see you regardless; Gorum;
Cayden Cailean, whose theology is available by the pint; the Green Faith; the clan's god, whom
you have never met; nothing, since Armasse, which is the fastest-growing faith in Mendev. What
is the gesture you make when you pass the temples, and do you mean it, and does it matter?

**What do you carry?** A zoic fetish that has protected you from everything except what
happened. A skull in a box, which is a relative. A banner you did not earn. A banner you did,
which is worse, because you know what it cost. A sword-and-sun badge on a coat that is not yours,
from a man who no longer needs the coat. A relic of the pirate days, for when the crusade is
over. A brewery token, the one currency that has held its value. Twelve years' service and a scar
you explain differently every time, because the truth was less interesting.

**What did the Third Crusade do to your family?** If you are native Mendevian or Sarkorian, the
answer is probably not "nothing." If you are crusader stock, the answer is probably in the other
column.

**What does the word "wardstone" mean to you now?** It meant a wall. Then it meant a wall that
could crack. Then it meant a wall that four strangers broke on purpose, and everyone cheered.
Work forward from there.

**What do you want from Drezen?** Pay. A wall. A name. A grave to visit. A god to find. A way
west. A way to never go west again. A quiet drink in a city with one brewery and no quiet.
Everyone in Drezen wants one of these and is going to get a different one.

### Naming by people

- **Native Mendevian** — Iobarian roots; sturdy, slightly Slavic-sounding, the names of people
  who expected to be here a while. *Hulrun Shappok, Irabeth Tirabade, Anevia, Sosiel Vaenic,
  Nestrin Alodae, Eterrius Sunnestier, Kamilo Dann, Trudean Delashaw, Jeskar Hinton, Tilda
  Merrow.*
- **Crusader stock** — southern names from anywhere: Taldan (*Galfrey, Aron Kir, Aldrich
  Thorne, Brannic Vayne, Dyre, Marcovina*), Chelaxian, Andoren, Varisian. A family name that is
  a place ("of Lauterbury") is a knightly affectation, and usually a place that has never heard
  of them.
- **Sarkorian / Kellid** — short and hard: *Dron, Gurog, Kronug, Belka, Inkit, Yala*; the clan
  name over the given name; a longer, stranger name for the notable: *Uloric Dziergas, Domora
  Hume, Khraigorr Half-Face, Tarnshiav, Widowknife, Farheaven.* Nobody in Sarkoris was ever
  called something they could not shout across a valley.
- **Mongrelmen of Neathholm** — single, plain, and given underground by people who did not
  expect anyone above to ask: *Lann, Chusul, Opoli, Chyrrik.*
- **Drezen-born** — whatever the demons let them be called. *Fenna Tusk* is the one example, and
  she seems to have chosen it herself.
